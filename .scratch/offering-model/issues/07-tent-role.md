# Decide what the tent actually is

Type: grilling
Status: open
Blocked by: 02

## Question

Is the pop-up event tent a rental, a service, a venue play, or a capability that
never appears on the menu at all?

It is the one asset that makes the larger events business plausible, and it is
currently unexamined. The options are genuinely different businesses:

- **Rental.** It goes out on its own, priced per day. Highest volume, lowest
  margin, and it puts Poppies into competition with established rental companies
  — including Poppies & Top Hats, which already trades on the same word in the
  same state.
- **Bundled capability.** It never sells alone; it makes larger balloon
  installations possible and justifies a higher-priced tier. Lowest risk, and it
  keeps the business one thing.
- **Venue play.** The tent plus a coastal location becomes the product, with
  balloons as decoration. The largest opportunity and by far the biggest leap —
  it changes what the business *is*, and probably what the website is.
- **Not an offering.** Owned equipment, used opportunistically, never marketed.

Judge against the constraint from ticket 03 answer 8: what it actually takes to
transport and erect, and whether that is a one-person job.

## Why this blocks

The growth sequence cannot be ordered without knowing whether the tent is step
two or a different business entirely.

## Comments

**2026-11-07: research input, not a decision.** The overnight market research
(`.scratch/ideas/`) bears directly on this ticket. The grilling is still paused
on its last open question (does the tent ever go out without balloons?), and
the choice between the prototype variants is still the owner's.

What the research adds:

- **Variant B, the backyard venue, has outside evidence behind it.**
  Venue-in-a-box businesses sell "skip the $3,000–15,000 venue" (a backyard
  wedding bundle at about $1,810 in Arizona; bell-tent pop-ups from about
  $1,300). Coast weddings typically run 25–165 guests at resorts or at
  *non-venues* (rental houses, beaches, lawns), and no event barns turned up on
  the central coast. (`research/02` venue-in-a-box; `research/03` §3)
- **Tent plus decor is rare.** Tent-rental companies don't style, and balloon
  vendors don't bring tents. One vendor, one insurance certificate and one
  permit is a real convenience. (`research/06` idea 13)
- **There are compliance costs the prototype didn't show.** A tent over 400 sq
  ft generally needs a fire-department permit and an NFPA 701
  flame-resistance certificate in Oregon. Venues typically ask for $1M/$2M
  general liability insurance with themselves named as additional insured.
  Beach structures need an OPRD special use permit ($100, applied for 30 days
  ahead, freestanding decor only). (`research/06` §6; `research/03` §3)
- **Rental-only (the case against variant C becoming a rental line):** nothing
  in the research favours a tent-only rental. Coast rental companies already
  rent tents; Big Tent Rents in Coos Bay goes up to 40×100.
- **The tent also fixes the weather problem.** Honest longevity guidance puts
  outdoor balloons at "the day of the event" in sun and wind, and several days
  under cover. Newport wind averages 23 km/h in summer. (`research/05` §1.1;
  `research/03` §6)
