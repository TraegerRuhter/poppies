# Pricing benchmarks for balloon installation work

Type: research
Status: resolved

## Question

What do balloon artists actually charge, and on what basis, in Oregon and the
wider Pacific Northwest?

Specifically:

- **Organic garlands** — the going rate per linear foot, and whether that rate
  changes with height, complexity, or indoor/outdoor placement.
- **Arches** — typical price ranges by span and type (full arch, half arch,
  sculptural).
- **Sculptural figure work** — how one-off sculptures get priced when there is no
  per-foot basis.
- **Add-ons that are commonly billed separately** — delivery, travel beyond a
  radius, on-site installation time, same-day teardown, rental of frames or
  stands, rush fees.
- **Minimums.** Whether a booking minimum is standard, and what it typically is.
- **The wedding premium**, if any — whether the same garland costs more for a
  wedding than a birthday, and how vendors justify it.

Report ranges with sources, not a single number. Note where the Oregon coast
differs from Portland — travel time to coastal venues is a real cost that inland
pricing will not reflect.

## Why this blocks

The pricing basis (per-foot, per-piece, package, quote-only) cannot be chosen
without knowing what the market already does. It is also the input the owner is
most likely to have an instinct about, so getting the benchmarks right makes her
ratification session productive rather than exploratory.

## Answer

Full findings: `../research/01-pricing-benchmarks.md`.

**Caveat that colours everything below: every direct page fetch was blocked by the
egress proxy, including The Knot and WeddingWire.** All figures rest on
search-result snippets, marked per-figure in the research file as attributed or
`[unattributed aggregate]`. Nothing was estimated. Re-running unblocked would
raise confidence materially.

**Pricing bases in use.** Per-linear-foot dominates organic garland nationally and
is the taught industry standard. Per-piece flat pricing dominates fixed-size
items. **Quote-only dominates the Oregon market specifically** — every Oregon
vendor found publishes nothing, or `$0.00` placeholder pages.

**Ranges.**

- Organic garland **$25–$42/linear ft**; classic technique **$5–$10/ft**. The gap
  reconciles exactly against the industry rule that organic uses triple the
  balloons and triple the labour — which makes it a defensible basis for setting a
  rate from first principles rather than copying a competitor.
- Arches **$100–$1,000+**. The cleanest read on the organic premium holds span
  constant: $350/$475 organic against $200/$250 classic at identical spans.
- **Sculpture is two different markets, not one.** Live twisting runs ~$150/hr
  (real Oregon data, Portland-weighted); commissioned display pieces run $250+,
  priced by build hours, with a life-size figure around $200 simple to $400 fine
  detail.
- Add-ons: delivery $125–$350, install $50–$200 or 20% of subtotal, strike
  $50–$200, outdoor surcharge $50–$350, frames $50–$75, rush 10% inside 7 days.
- **Minimums $250–$695, clustering at $500.** The nearest geographic comparables
  are the two Seattle anchors at $500 and $695.

**Three findings that change the picture.**

1. **No Oregon coast pricing exists to benchmark against.** No coastal balloon
   business publishes rates. This is opportunity rather than obstacle — there is
   no local anchor dragging her down, and coastal clients' reference prices will
   be whatever they find searching, which is Portland and national.
2. **Inland competition reaches the north-central coast but gates it on job
   size.** Salem Balloons names Lincoln City, Depoe Bay and Neskowin, travelling
   beyond its area only for larger events. Nobody found reaches Astoria or the
   south coast. The competitive picture is not uniform along the coastline.
3. **The wedding premium is a scope gap, not a rate gap.** No vendor publishes two
   rates for identical work. The one hard operational difference is lead time —
   2–6 months for weddings against ~2 weeks general — which for a solo operator
   with finite Saturdays makes *the price of holding a date* the most defensible
   justification available.

**Assumption flagged for the owner, and it is the sharp one:** whether her figure
work is commissioned display sculpture or live twisting. They price completely
differently and only she can settle it.

**Unresolved:** no Oregon per-foot rate, no coastal travel fee from any vendor, no
marketplace price bands (both domains blocked), and **no source anywhere publishes
a height-tiered rate** — so this ticket's premise that per-foot varies with height
is unsupported.
