# Map: Poppies offering model

Label: wayfinder:map
Status: open
Date: 2026-08-12

## Destination

A written proposal for Poppies' offering model — the service list, how each is
packaged and priced, and the rule for how a new offering gets added — concrete
enough for the owner to react to and ratify in a single sitting.

Reaching the destination means nothing is left to *decide* before the proposal is
put in front of her. It does not mean the model is final; her ratification is a
separate act, outside this map.

## Notes

**Domain.** Poppies is a balloon art business on the Oregon coast — sculptural
figure work, arches, garlands — with access to a large pop-up event tent and
ambitions toward weddings and larger events.

**The owner is not available during this effort.** She is the decision-maker for
every question here, so this map produces a *proposal*, not decisions. Two
standing consequences:

1. Every ticket outputs a recommendation **with its options and rationale
   intact**, so she can disagree with the reasoning rather than just the answer.
2. **Questions for her are first-class output.** A proposal built without her will
   encode assumptions she would never have made — how she likes to work, what she
   refuses to do, what she is fastest at. Each ticket ends with what it had to
   assume, gathered into the final proposal as an explicit list.

**Related context, not restated here:**

- `.scratch/website/decisions.md` — the website's settled technical and design
  decisions. The site's content model is blocked on this map.
- `.scratch/naming/poppies-name-collision.md` — name research; four Oregon
  businesses already trade on "Poppies," three touching weddings.

**Skills.** `/research` for outside facts, `/prototype` for anything where "what
should this look like" is the question, `/grilling` for the rest.

## Decisions so far

<!-- one line per closed ticket -->

- [Pricing benchmarks for balloon installation work](issues/01-pricing-benchmarks.md)
  — per-linear-foot is the national standard (organic $25–$42/ft, classic $5–$10/ft)
  but **quote-only dominates Oregon**; minimums cluster at $500; sculpture is two
  different markets (live twisting ~$150/hr vs commissioned pieces $250+); **no
  coastal vendor publishes anything**, so there is no local anchor to be dragged
  down by. Snippet-sourced — the proxy blocked every direct fetch.
- [How comparable businesses package their offerings](issues/02-packaging-patterns.md)
  — **two tracks divided by a stated minimum**, figure work outside both; no
  example found of a balloon business splitting brands for weddings; occasion pages
  are navigation, not packaging, which **validates the website's doorway split as
  register-only**; the real bifurcation is buy-it-now vs commission-it.

## Not yet specified

- **Capacity and seasonality** — how many installations she can physically deliver
  in a month, and how coast wedding season concentrates demand. Needs her, and
  shapes whether growth means more offerings or higher prices.
- **What happens to the balloon work if events become the larger business.**
  Premature until the growth sequence exists.

## Out of scope

- **Entity structure, assumed-business-name registration, and liability
  insurance.** Ruled out at charting — this map is the offering model, not the
  business's legal setup.

  **Flagged risk:** liability insurance can gate the wedding offering outright,
  since many venues will not admit an uninsured vendor. If that turns out to be
  true, the growth sequence this map produces may need redrawing as a fresh
  effort.
