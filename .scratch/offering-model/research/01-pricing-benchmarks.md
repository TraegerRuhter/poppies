## Method and reliability warning — read this first

**No web page in this research was successfully fetched.** This session's network
egress proxy returned `403` on every balloon-vendor domain attempted
(`salemballoonsandflowers.com`, `yteevents.com`, `theknot.com`, `weddingwire.com`,
`fairpoppers.com`, `lushballoons.com`, `balloonartisan.com`, and others — the
block is broad, not vendor-specific; even `en.wikipedia.org` was refused).

Every number below therefore comes from **search-result snippets**, not from a
page I read. That has three consequences worth holding onto:

1. **Snippets can be stale.** A "$25/ft" indexed last year may not be this year's
   rate.
2. **Attribution is weaker than a citation normally implies.** Where the search
   engine named the vendor alongside the number, I attribute it. Where it
   returned an aggregate across several results without naming which one, I mark
   it `[unattributed aggregate]` and you should treat it as a shape, not a fact.
3. **Nothing here is verified against a live quote.** Before any of this becomes
   Poppies' price list, at least a handful of these should be re-checked by
   loading the pages directly, and ideally two or three by mystery-shopping a
   quote.

Geography is labelled on every source. **Very little published pricing exists for
Oregon at all, and none was found for the Oregon coast.** That absence is itself
one of the more useful findings — see the Oregon coast section.

## Summary: what pricing bases are actually in use

Four bases are in circulation. They are not competing alternatives — most working
vendors run two or three at once, applied to different parts of the same job.

**1. Per linear foot — dominates organic garland, and is the industry's default
teaching.** This is the one basis with genuine cross-market consistency. Balloon
HQ, the long-running industry FAQ resource, presents linear-foot pricing as the
standard unit for arches and garlands and quotes classic-decor anchors in it
([Balloon HQ, Pricing Balloon Decor](https://balloonhq.com/faq/deco_pricing/)).
Retail vendors publishing rate cards almost universally publish a per-foot number
for garland. If Poppies publishes anything, this is the number the market will
expect to see.

**2. Per piece / flat price — dominates anything with a fixed size.** Standard
arches on stands, columns, backdrop panels, number-and-letter arches. Vendors
who price per foot for garland still price these flat, because the size is not
the customer's variable. Examples: Made Ya Look's standard arch sizes, Seattle
Balloon Artist's column and backdrop prices (both below).

**3. Package with a starting-at anchor — the wedding/large-event presentation.**
Seattle Balloon Artist publishes "packages start at $695"
([Seattle Balloon Artist, Packages](https://www.seattleballoonartist.com/packages)).
This is a minimum wearing a menu's clothing.

**4. Quote-only — dominates the Oregon market specifically.** Every Oregon
balloon business I found either published no prices at all or published
`$0.00` placeholder product pages requiring configuration. Balloon Artisan
(Portland) routes entirely to a quote form and states pricing depends on "travel
distance, set up times, material costs, and design/installation complexity"
([Balloon Artisan, Large Balloon Arch and Installation
Pricing](https://www.balloonartisan.com/large-balloon-arch-and-installation-pricing)).
Salem Balloons and Flowers, the one Oregon vendor found who explicitly serves
coastal towns, publishes no rates
([Salem Balloons and Flowers](https://www.salemballoonsandflowers.com/)).

**The pattern that matters for Poppies:** per-foot is how the *category* is
priced nationally, but quote-only is how the *Oregon* market presents itself.
Published per-foot rates cluster in states with denser balloon markets. This is
a real strategic fork, not a data gap — publishing a rate would make Poppies
unusually transparent for this market, which cuts both ways.

The structural formula behind all of it, from the industry side: Balloon HQ
cites the QBN (Qualatex Balloon Network) formula as
`SELLING PRICE = (MATERIALS + LABOR + EXPENSES) / (1 - (overhead% + profit%))`
([Balloon HQ, Pricing Balloon Decor](https://balloonhq.com/faq/deco_pricing/)).
Per-foot rates are an output of that, not an input.

### Published per-foot rates

| ~$5/linear ft | String of Pearls (classic technique, not organic) | [Balloon HQ](https://balloonhq.com/faq/deco_pricing/) | National guidance |
| ~$6/linear ft | Quicklink arch (classic technique) | [Balloon HQ](https://balloonhq.com/faq/deco_pricing/) | National guidance |
| $20/ft and up | Garland using 5"–16" balloons | [FairPoppers Balloon Boutique](https://fairpoppers.com/pages/size-price-guide) | Not PNW |
| $25/linear ft | Organic garland | [Lush Balloons](https://www.lushballoons.com/pages/general-pricing-guide) | Not PNW |

| $42/linear ft | Extra lush organic garland with florals | [Lush Balloons](https://www.lushballoons.com/pages/general-pricing-guide) | Not PNW |
| $27/ft | Standard and deluxe colors | [Party & Co.](https://www.partyand.co/product-page/balloons-garlands-w-o-a-stand) | Not PNW |
| $37/ft | Custom, double-stuffed, or all-metallic | [Party & Co.](https://www.partyand.co/product-page/balloons-garlands-w-o-a-stand) | Not PNW |
| $35/ft | Organic arches and garlands | [Made Ya Look Balloons](https://madeyalookballoons.com/pricing/) | Charlotte, NC |
| $10/ft | Basic linking-arch style | [Made Ya Look Balloons](https://madeyalookballoons.com/pricing/) | Charlotte, NC |
| $25/ft → $30/ft → $35/ft | Standard → design including chrome/metallic → exclusively chrome | [The Balloon Babe](https://theballoonbabe.com/pricing-menu) | SW Florida (Sarasota) |

**The working band for organic garland is roughly $25–$42 per linear foot**,
with $25/ft appearing repeatedly as the entry point for organic and the $35–$42
band reserved for florals, all-chrome, or double-stuffing. Classic (non-organic)
technique sits an order of magnitude lower, at $5–$10/ft — which is the single
most important structural fact in this table, because it says the rate is
pricing *technique and labor*, not balloons.

Balloon HQ states the trade's original rule of thumb directly: when organic decor
emerged, it was priced at **"triple the balloons and triple the labor of classic
decor"** ([Balloon HQ](https://balloonhq.com/faq/deco_pricing/)). That
reconciles the $5–$10 classic band with the $25–$35 organic band almost exactly,
and is a defensible basis for setting a rate from first principles rather than
copying a competitor.

`[unattributed aggregate]` One search synthesis gave an outer envelope of
"$5 per linear foot for simplified helium arches" to "$90 per linear foot for
some organic arch displays." The $90 end was not traceable to a named vendor and
should not be relied on.

### Does the per-foot rate vary with height, complexity, indoor/outdoor?

**Complexity — yes, and this is the primary driver.** Every vendor publishing
tiers tiers on materials and technique, not on size:

- Foils, confetti fills, jumbo balloons, greenery and florals all step the rate
  up ([Lush Balloons](https://www.lushballoons.com/pages/general-pricing-guide);
  [Party & Co.](https://www.partyand.co/product-page/balloons-garlands-w-o-a-stand)).
- Double-stuffing and foils are called out by Balloon HQ as increasing cost
  significantly ([Balloon HQ](https://balloonhq.com/faq/deco_pricing/)).
- Custom palettes, multiple textures, neon signs and themed builds are described
  as taking more time and materials `[unattributed aggregate]`.

**Outdoor — yes, but as a flat surcharge, not a rate change.** This is the clean
finding:

- **+$100** if an arch is to be used outdoors — [Made Ya Look
  Balloons](https://madeyalookballoons.com/pricing/) (Charlotte, NC).
- **$50–$350** outdoor setup charge, for stability and weather resistance —
  [Balloon HQ](https://balloonhq.com/blog/how-much-does-a-balloon-arch-cost/).

Note how directly this bears on a coastal business: wind exposure is the norm,
not the exception, at Oregon coast venues. A flat outdoor surcharge is the
market-standard instrument for it.

**Height — found only indirectly.** No vendor was found publishing a per-foot
rate that steps with height. What was found:

- The Balloon Babe's *delivery* fee increases if the display is larger than 15'
  ([The Balloon Babe](https://theballoonbabe.com/pricing-menu)) — height priced
  into logistics rather than into the garland rate.
- High ceiling heights are named as a design consideration affecting price, but
  without a published number `[unattributed aggregate]`.

I could not find a published height-tiered per-foot rate anywhere. See "what I
could not determine."

([Balloon HQ](https://balloonhq.com/blog/how-much-does-a-balloon-arch-cost/)).

**Flat prices by span, where published:**

| $475 | Spanning a 6-foot serving table | Organic | [Made Ya Look](https://madeyalookballoons.com/pricing/) (NC) |
| $200 | Large enough for 2 people | String of pearls | [Made Ya Look](https://madeyalookballoons.com/pricing/) (NC) |

The Made Ya Look pairs are the most instructive row set in this whole document:
**the same two spans, priced organic vs classic, come out at $350/$475 vs
$200/$250.** Organic carries roughly a 1.75×–1.9× multiple over classic at
identical span. That is a cleaner read on the organic premium than any per-foot
comparison, because span is held constant.

**PNW-specific arch anchors (sparse):**

- Balloon Artisan, Portland OR: "some designs start around $500, while others go
  up into the thousands"; "most large balloon installations range between
  $10k–$30k" ([Balloon Artisan](https://www.balloonartisan.com/large-balloon-arch-and-installation-pricing)).
  The $10k–$30k figure is for large-scale commercial installation work and is
  not a comparable to anything Poppies currently does — treat it as the ceiling
  of the category, not of the market she is in.
- Seattle Balloon Artist: Organic Balloon Column **$230**; Grab & Go Garland
  **$80**; backdrops from **$175**; rectangle panels from **$225**; 8.5 ft
  backdrops from **$350** ([Seattle Balloon
  Artist](https://www.seattleballoonartist.com/store)).
- PDX Parties publishes arch *dimensions* but no prices in indexed snippets:
  standard arch ~8' tall × 11' wide with 6.5' clearance; larger option ~10' tall
  × 16' wide with 8.5' clearance ([PDX
  Parties](https://pdxparties.com/balloon-arrangements/balloon-arches/full-arch/)).
  Useful as a size vocabulary even without the price.

I found **no source distinguishing full arch from half arch pricing**. The
industry appears to price by span and technique, with "half arch" not treated as
a named price category.

## Sculptural figure work — how one-offs get priced

Two entirely separate markets share the word "sculpture," and they price
differently. Poppies' figure work likely straddles both.

### A. Live twisting / entertainment — priced per hour

This is the better-documented of the two, and it is the one with real Oregon
data.

| Rate | Terms | Source | Region |
|---|---|---|---|
| $150 | Average quote to hire a balloon twister | [The Bash](https://www.thebash.com/search/balloon-twister-portland-or) | **Portland, OR** |
| $195 | Average cost to hire a balloon twister | [The Bash](https://www.thebash.com/search/balloon-twister-portland-or) | **Oregon statewide** |
| $350 | One artist, two hours custom twisting, full setup and cleanup | [Twist PDX](https://www.twistpdx.com/) | **Portland, OR** |
| $129.99/hr | + tax and travel | Kiddy's Kingdom / Phillips Celebrations, via [GigSalad](https://www.gigsalad.com/Variety/Balloon-Artist/OR/Portland) | **Portland, OR** |
| $150/hr, 2 hr minimum | Balloons only | via [GigSalad](https://www.gigsalad.com/Variety/Balloon-Artist/OR/Portland) | **Portland, OR** |
| $200 for 1 hr, or $150/hr for 1.5+ hrs | Declining rate with duration | via [GigSalad](https://www.gigsalad.com/Variety/Balloon-Artist/OR/Portland) | **Portland, OR** |
| $75–$150/hr | Yielding 15–100 finished balloons, i.e. **$1.50–$10 per sculpture** | [CostHelper](https://activities.costhelper.com/balloon-animals.html) | National |

Note the shape: **$150/hr is the Portland gravity well**, with a premium for
short bookings ($200 for a single hour) and a two-hour minimum common. Twist PDX
bundles setup and cleanup into the $350 rather than billing them out.

### B. Display sculpture / commissioned figure work — priced per piece, by build time

No per-foot basis exists here. The published logic is consistently **size ×
intricacy × build hours**, quoted per commission:

- Sculptures **start at $250** for smaller designs; larger and more detailed
  begin higher; complex branded or oversized installations are custom-quote only
  `[unattributed aggregate across vendor sculpture pages]`.
- **Life-size human figure: ~$200 in simple detail, ~$400 in fine detail** with
  woven clothing and a detailed face — [Jonah's
  Twisters](https://jonahstwisters.com/custom-sculptures/) (Boston, MA). This is
  the single most useful data point in the section, because it isolates
  *detail level at constant size* and prices it at 2×.
- **Budget-anchored scaling:** ~$40 yields a 3-foot detailed airplane; ~$1,300
  yields a 40-foot jetliner — [Jonah's
  Twisters](https://jonahstwisters.com/custom-sculptures/).
- Overall range "$100 to several thousand dollars, depending on size and
  intricacy"; "pricing is reflective of size and time to build the piece"
  `[unattributed aggregate]`.
- Delivery is billed separately on custom sculptures depending on event location,
  and long lead times are requested for color sourcing and specialty materials
  `[unattributed aggregate]`.

**The practical takeaway:** the trade prices one-off sculpture by quoting build
hours against a shop rate and adding materials, then presenting a single
per-piece number. The customer never sees the hours. The "life-size figure:
$200 simple / $400 fine detail" pair is the closest thing found to a published
detail-tier, and it is a usable model — a two- or three-tier detail ladder at a
given size, rather than an open-ended custom quote every time.

## Separately-billed add-ons

This is where the market is most consistent, and where a one-person business
most often under-charges. Every figure below is a **separate line item**, not
included in the decor price.

**Delivery**
- **$125–$350** — [Balloon HQ](https://balloonhq.com/blog/how-much-does-a-balloon-arch-cost/).
- **Minimum $25, or $1.50 per mile** from the home office; the fee increases if
  the event is more than 20 miles away or the display is larger than 15' —
  [The Balloon Babe](https://theballoonbabe.com/pricing-menu) (SW FL).
- **Calculated based on the delivery city** — [Seattle Balloon
  Artist](https://www.seattleballoonartist.com/packages). A city-band model
  rather than a mileage model.

**On-site installation**
- **$50–$200** typical — [Balloon HQ](https://balloonhq.com/blog/how-much-does-a-balloon-arch-cost/).
- **$100 per hour** for assembling and installing decor on-site `[unattributed aggregate]`.
- **20% of the display subtotal** — [The Balloon
  Babe](https://theballoonbabe.com/pricing-menu). A percentage-of-job model
  rather than an hourly one; scales automatically with job size.

**Teardown / strike (same-day or next-day)**
- **$50–$200** — [Balloon HQ](https://balloonhq.com/blog/how-much-does-a-balloon-arch-cost/).
- **From $55, depending on when you want it to occur** `[unattributed aggregate]`.
- The strike fee covers "returning to pop balloons and haul away framing"
  `[unattributed aggregate]`. One vendor's terms specify that if the client
  then handles teardown themselves, the paid strike fee is forfeited — the time
  was reserved `[unattributed aggregate]`.
- Advice repeatedly given to *customers*: ask for an "all-in" price that
  explicitly includes delivery and strike, to avoid surprises. Worth knowing —
  it is what informed clients will ask Poppies for.

**Outdoor surcharge**
- **$50–$350** — [Balloon HQ](https://balloonhq.com/blog/how-much-does-a-balloon-arch-cost/).
- **+$100** flat — [Made Ya Look](https://madeyalookballoons.com/pricing/).

**Frame / stand / backdrop rental**
- **From $60** for backdrop stands, hoop stands and Chiara frames `[unattributed aggregate]`.
- **$50–$75 per rental** for stands and frames `[unattributed aggregate]`.
- **From $450** for higher-end hoop and A-frame rentals `[unattributed aggregate]`.
- **Late return: $20+ per day** beyond 3 days after the event, plus replacement
  cost for damage `[unattributed aggregate]`.
- Salem Balloons and Flowers sells "garland with frame rental" as a distinct
  product line ([Salem Balloons and
  Flowers](https://www.salemballoonsandflowers.com/products/organic-balloon-garland-with-frame)) —
  Oregon evidence that frame-inclusive packaging is a recognised product here,
  though the price rendered as $0.00 in indexed snippets.

**Rush fees**
- **10% rush fee for orders placed within 7 days of the event** — [Party Baby
  Seattle](https://partybabyseattle.com/seattle-balloon-decor/). **This is the
  only PNW rush policy found with a number, and a percentage rush fee is a
  cleaner instrument than a flat one for a solo operator.**
- **$50–$800** for orders with less than 72 hours' notice, covering expedited
  materials and labor `[unattributed aggregate]`.
- Rush fees inside 7 days determined by theme and cost of expedited sourcing
  `[unattributed aggregate]`.

**Deposits**
- Non-refundable deposit to reserve the date, balance due before install; some
  vendors take **50%** `[unattributed aggregate]`.
- One vendor requires bookings invoiced **$800 or more** to be booked 30 days
  prior — [My Balloon Obsession](https://myballoonobsession.com/service-agreement).

**Seasonal premium**
- One source reports a **10–20% premium** during the November–April busy season
  for weddings, corporate and holiday events `[unattributed aggregate]`. Flagged
  because the months are wrong for coastal Oregon wedding season — this is
  almost certainly a warm-climate market's calendar and should not be carried
  over. The *mechanism* (a peak-season premium) transfers; the months do not.

## Booking minimums

**A minimum is standard practice**, expressed in one of three ways.

**As a dollar floor on the whole booking:**
- **$500 minimum rental**, all rentals are 4 hours, no proration for less time —
  [Party Baby Seattle](https://partybabyseattle.com/seattle-balloon-decor/) **(Seattle, WA — nearest-market PNW anchor)**.
- **$500 minimum on custom designs** — [The Balloon
  Babe](https://theballoonbabe.com/pricing-menu) (SW FL).
- **$600 order minimum for Sunday installations** — [Gidi
  Balloons](https://gidiballoons.com/) (NJ). A day-of-week minimum; interesting
  as a tool for protecting a specific day.

**As a "starting at" package anchor, which functions as a minimum:**
- **Packages start at $695** — [Seattle Balloon
  Artist](https://www.seattleballoonartist.com/packages) **(Seattle/Tacoma, WA)**.
- **On-site installation starts at $250 + tax and delivery** — [Delloons
  Boutique](https://www.delloons.com/pages/faq).

**As a minimum length rather than a minimum dollar:**
- **15 ft ($375) order minimum on all custom installations** — [Gidi
  Balloons](https://gidiballoons.com/) (NJ). Note this implies a $25/ft rate and
  is the cleanest example of a length-minimum expressed in the same unit as the
  price.

**As a geography-gated minimum — the one most relevant to Poppies:**
- M & M Balloon Co. of Seattle serves its core cities normally, with "other
  areas considered on a case-by-case basis **when meeting minimum order
  requirements**" ([M & M Balloon Co.](https://www.mmballoons.com/)). The
  minimum is the gate that makes distant work viable — exactly the instrument a
  coastal-radius business needs.

**Working band: $250–$695, clustering at $500.** The two Seattle data points
($500 and $695) are the closest geographic comparables available and both sit at
or above the middle of that band.

## The Oregon coast specifically

### The central finding: there is no published coastal pricing to benchmark against

I searched for balloon businesses in Astoria, Seaside, Cannon Beach, Tillamook,
Lincoln City, Depoe Bay, Newport, Florence, Coos Bay and Bandon. What exists:

- **Float Balloon Bar LC** (Lincoln City) — custom garlands and bouquets with
  delivery. **Facebook page only; no website, no published pricing**
  ([Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/p/Float-Balloon-Bar-LC-100063909591030/)).
- **Balloons In Motion** — publishes town-level delivery landing pages for
  [Astoria](https://balloonsinmotion.com/astoria-or-balloon-delivery/) and
  [Lincoln City](https://balloonsinmotion.com/lincoln-city-or-balloon-delivery/),
  covering "grand installations to simple bouquets." **No pricing found on
  either.**
- **No balloon business found in Newport, Coos Bay, Tillamook, Bandon, or the
  Astoria/Seaside/Cannon Beach cluster with published rates.**
- [Oregon Coast Weddings](https://oregoncoastweddings.com/) maintains a coastal
  vendor directory running as far south as Coos Bay, Bandon, Gold Beach and
  Brookings, but its balloon coverage appears thin to absent — florists dominate
  the decor listings.

**Read this as opportunity, not as a dead end.** Poppies is being priced into a
market with essentially no published local comparables. That means (a) no local
price anchor is dragging her down, and (b) coastal clients' reference prices are
whatever they find searching, which will be Portland and national rates.

### Who currently reaches the coast, and how they frame it

**Salem Balloons and Flowers** is the significant one. It explicitly names
**Lincoln City, Depoe Bay and Neskowin** as "select Oregon Coast destinations"
served, alongside its Willamette Valley core (Albany, Corvallis, Independence,
Monmouth, Dallas, Woodburn, Silverton, Sublimity, Stayton, McMinnville,
Wilsonville, Eugene and greater Portland), and states it is "often available to
travel beyond their standard service area" for **larger events, corporate
activations, weddings and specialty installations** ([Salem Balloons and
Flowers](https://www.salemballoonsandflowers.com/)).

Two things follow. First, **the inland competition reaches the north-central
coast but gates it on job size** — the same instrument M & M Balloon uses in
Seattle. Second, **nobody found reaches Astoria or the south coast from
inland.** The competitive picture is not uniform along the coastline.

### How travel gets billed, and why inland rates won't cover it

No coastal travel fee was found published by any balloon vendor. The mechanisms
in use elsewhere, which is what there is to work from:

- **Per-mile with a floor:** minimum $25 or **$1.50/mile**, escalating beyond 20
  miles — [The Balloon Babe](https://theballoonbabe.com/pricing-menu). Note that
  $1.50/mile is roughly **double the IRS mileage rate of $0.70/mile** cited in
  wedding-vendor travel discussion
  ([WeddingWire forum](https://www.weddingwire.com/wedding-forums/travel-fee-for-vendors/c0e72d1aa8429ac3.html)) —
  the gap is labor time, not vehicle cost.
- **By delivery city:** delivery, setup and teardown "calculated based on the
  delivery city" — [Seattle Balloon
  Artist](https://www.seattleballoonartist.com/packages).
- **Free within a radius, billed beyond it:** the common wedding-vendor pattern —
  no travel fee inside a mile radius of the office, charged outside it
  ([WeddingWire forum](https://www.weddingwire.com/wedding-forums/travel-fee-for-vendors/c0e72d1aa8429ac3.html)).
- **Travel distance named as a pricing input** rather than a fee: Balloon
  Artisan (Portland) lists "travel distance" first among the factors setting
  installation price ([Balloon
  Artisan](https://www.balloonartisan.com/large-balloon-arch-and-installation-pricing)).
- **Travel folded in but flagged:** Twist PDX serves Portland metro and
  surrounding cities and notes events outside the area "may include a small
  travel fee" ([Twist PDX](https://www.twistpdx.com/faq)); Kiddy's Kingdom quotes
  $129.99/hr "+ Tax & Travel"
  ([GigSalad](https://www.gigsalad.com/Variety/Balloon-Artist/OR/Portland)).

**Why Portland rates structurally under-recover on the coast.** Coast Range
crossings are slow, two-lane, weather-exposed and offer no route redundancy;
Highway 101 is the only north–south spine, so serving Astoria and Bandon from
one base are not comparable jobs. A Portland vendor's install day absorbs travel
into a dense schedule of nearby jobs. A coastal vendor cannot — **a single
install can consume the day**. Any per-foot rate imported from Portland
therefore carries a Portland assumption about jobs-per-day that does not hold
here. I state this as reasoning, not as sourced fact: **no source was found
quantifying it, and it should be validated against her actual drive times and
job cadence rather than accepted from me.**

The market's own answer to this problem is visible in the M & M Balloon and
Salem Balloons patterns: **a minimum that rises with distance**, so that distant
work is either worth the day or declined. That is a stronger instrument than a
mileage fee for a one-person business, because mileage recovers fuel while a
minimum recovers the *day*.

### Same-day teardown on the coast

Worth flagging as a distinct exposure. Strike fees of $50–$200
([Balloon HQ](https://balloonhq.com/blog/how-much-does-a-balloon-arch-cost/))
assume a short return trip. A same-day teardown at a coastal venue is a **second
full round trip**, and at $50–$200 the national band does not remotely cover it.
No source was found addressing teardown pricing at distance.

## The wedding premium

### Does the same garland cost more at a wedding?

**No vendor was found publishing two different rates for identical work by event
type.** Balloon Artisan maintains separate pricing landing pages for
[weddings](https://www.balloonartisan.com/wedding-balloon-arch-and-decor-pricing),
[birthdays](https://www.balloonartisan.com/birthday-balloon-arch-and-decor-pricing),
[baby showers](https://www.balloonartisan.com/baby-shower-balloon-arch-and-decor-pricing)
and [corporate](https://www.balloonartisan.com/corporate-balloon-arch-and-decor-pricing),
but **none of them publishes a number** — the segmentation is presentational, and
the price is a quote in every case. That is itself the finding: the trade
segments its *marketing* by event type far more than it segments its *rate card*.

### The premium shows up as scope, not as rate

Where wedding and birthday figures appear side by side, the gap is real but it
is a gap in what gets built:

- Wedding balloon decor from **~$80** for simple tulle balloons to **$800+** for
  full wedding arches with additional decor; elaborate weddings and corporate
  events **$500–$2,000** depending on scale and customization —
  [Fashion Balloons](https://fashion-balloons.com/balloon-decoration-cost-guide/)
  (Florida; national blog framing).
- Birthday party balloon arrangement **$60–$150** — [Fashion
  Balloons](https://fashion-balloons.com/balloon-decoration-cost-guide/).
- CostHelper's wedding-specific data: table decorations and smaller pieces
  **$15–$100+**; floor-standing columns/arrangements **$40–$110+ each**; arches
  **$75–$500+**
  ([CostHelper](https://weddings.costhelper.com/decorative-balloons-cost.html)).
- Zola lists at least one balloon vendor whose "prices start at $75" for wedding
  work ([Zola](https://www.zola.com/wedding-vendors/wedding-extras/texas-balloon-co-luxury-balloons-events)) —
  a reminder that the wedding label alone does not float the floor.

The comparison is not like-for-like: a $60–$150 birthday arrangement and an
$800 wedding arch are different objects. **I found no evidence of a per-foot
rate that changes because the event is a wedding.**

### How vendors justify wedding pricing

Four justifications recur, all from non-PNW sources and all `[unattributed
aggregate]` except where linked:

1. **Design register.** A wedding garland "calls for refined tones and a cleaner
   silhouette that complements, rather than competes with, the rest of the
   decor," where a birthday garland uses bold contrast, chrome accents and
   number balloons ([Alina Balloon](https://alinaballoon.com/balloon-garlands/),
   Los Angeles). Restraint is harder and reads as more expensive — this is the
   most substantive of the four, and it maps directly onto the register split
   already noted in `map.md`.
2. **Scale and sophistication.** Weddings and corporate events are simply bigger
   builds ([Fashion Balloons](https://fashion-balloons.com/balloon-decoration-cost-guide/)).
3. **Expertise and environmental judgment.** Professional garland work "requires
   specialized equipment, trained technique, and an understanding of how
   balloons behave across different environments and over the course of an
   event" ([The Balloon Guy LA](https://www.theballoonguyla.com/blog/how-much-is-a-balloon-garland/)).
   Directly applicable to windy coastal venues.
4. **Full-service relief.** Build, delivery, setup and removal all handled, so
   the client can attend their own event `[unattributed aggregate]`.

**Booking lead time is the one hard operational difference by event type:**
weddings are advised at **2–3 months** ([Balloon
HQ](https://balloonhq.com/weddings/)) to **3–6 months**
([Balloons Lane](https://balloonslane.com/wedding-balloon/)) ahead, against
Salem Balloons' general guidance of **at least 2 weeks** ([Salem Balloons and
Flowers](https://www.salemballoonsandflowers.com/)) and Party Baby Seattle's
**2–3 months** ([Party Baby Seattle](https://partybabyseattle.com/seattle-balloon-decor/)).
The wedding premium may be better understood as **the price of holding a date
for months**, which is a genuinely different product from a two-week birthday
turnaround — and for a one-person business with finite Saturdays, that opportunity
cost is the most defensible justification of the four.

## What I could not determine

Stated plainly, with no estimates substituted.

1. **Any Oregon vendor's published per-linear-foot garland rate.** Not one. Every
   Oregon business found routes to a quote or renders `$0.00` placeholder
   product pages. **The entire per-foot table above is out-of-region.**

2. **Any Oregon coast pricing whatsoever.** No coastal balloon business publishes
   rates. The two identified (Float Balloon Bar LC, Balloons In Motion) have no
   public prices.

3. **Any coastal travel fee, from anyone.** Salem Balloons serves Lincoln City,
   Depoe Bay and Neskowin but publishes no travel charge for doing so. I have no
   sourced basis for what the coast premium currently is, or whether one is
   charged at all.

4. **Whether a height-tiered per-foot rate exists.** No vendor found publishes
   one. Height appears only as a delivery-fee trigger (>15' at The Balloon Babe)
   and as an unquantified design consideration. The ticket's premise that rate
   varies with height is **not confirmed by any source found.**

5. **Full arch vs half arch as distinct price categories.** No source
   distinguishes them. The market prices by span and technique.

6. **The Knot / WeddingWire price bands for balloon vendors.** Both domains are
   egress-blocked, and searches restricted to them surfaced only venue, florist
   and rental listings — no balloon-vendor price bands. **The marketplace band
   data the ticket asked for could not be obtained.**

7. **Any like-for-like wedding-vs-birthday rate differential.** The wedding gap
   documented above is a scope gap. Whether vendors quietly quote weddings higher
   for identical work is not answerable from published sources.

8. **Salem Balloons and Flowers' actual price list** — the most competitively
   relevant single document for Poppies, since it is the one Oregon vendor
   serving both her market and her coastline. Egress-blocked; product pages
   rendered as `$0.00` in snippets.

9. **Current-year validity of every number here.** No page was fetched; all
   figures are snippet-derived and undated.

## Suggested next steps

Not decisions — the ticket's scope ends at benchmarks. But three things would
materially firm this up, in order of value:

1. **Re-run this from an unblocked network.** Priority fetches: Salem Balloons
   and Flowers' product pages, Seattle Balloon Artist's `/pricing`, and The Knot
   / WeddingWire balloon-vendor listings for Portland and coastal Oregon.
2. **Mystery-shop two quotes** — one Portland vendor for a coastal venue, one
   Salem Balloons quote for Lincoln City. That is the only way to get the coastal
   travel number, and it is the number this research most conspicuously lacks.
3. **Price from the QBN formula rather than from comparables.** Given how thin
   Oregon comparables are, building the rate from her own materials, labor and
   drive time and then sanity-checking it against the $25–$42/ft national band is
   more defensible than importing a Portland number.

## Assumptions this research had to make

Per `map.md`, assumptions are first-class output and belong in the final proposal.

- **That out-of-region per-foot rates are transferable to Oregon as a starting
  band.** They may not be; Balloon HQ explicitly notes regional variation, and
  every specific rate found is from a denser, warmer, or higher-cost market.
- **That "organic garland" means the same build in every source.** Vendors use
  the term loosely, and technique differences of the kind that separate $10/ft
  from $35/ft may be hiding inside it.
- **That her figure work is closer to commissioned display sculpture than to
  live twisting entertainment.** The two price completely differently ($250+
  per piece vs $150/hr), and which one she is in changes the answer materially.
  **This is a question for her, not something research can settle.**
- **That coastal travel is a cost she wants recovered explicitly** (fee or
  minimum) rather than absorbed into a higher blended rate. Both are legitimate;
  they imply very different price lists.
