# 07: Pricing and unit economics

Date: 2026-11-07. Status: **research input, not decisions.** Builds on
`../../offering-model/research/01-pricing-benchmarks.md` (cited below as
**R01**), which it extends rather than repeats.

## Sourcing caveat (read first)

- **Every figure comes from search-result snippets.** No page was fetched, as in
  R01. A snippet can be stale, can be the search engine's paraphrase, and can
  attach a number to the wrong product on a busy page.
- **Attribution.** Where a snippet named the business, I name it. Where it gave
  a number from a booking form or a page with no clear owner, I mark it
  `[unattributed]`. Treat those as showing the market's shape, not as facts.
- **Confidence:** **H** means a primary source, consistent across results (e.g.
  the IRS). **M** means a named business's own page, read from a snippet.
  **L** means a secondary, unattributed, SEO-style or vendor-affiliated source.
- **Geography.** Published balloon prices are still almost absent in Oregon.
  Every Oregon balloon vendor found quotes on request (R01). The PNW anchors
  below are mostly *adjacent* products: Portland tent rentals, Portland and
  Seattle proposal packages, and Seattle balloon items carried over from R01.
- **Dates.** Most prices carry no visible date. Treat them as "2024–2026,
  roughly".

### 2d. Labour time

- **16.5 paid minutes per foot** (≈2.75 h per 10 ft) in a balloon-business pricing
  spreadsheet's organic example. Its fully loaded cost comes to **$14.24/ft** at
  $25/h, with 10% spare balloons for pops
  ([Etsy pricing spreadsheet](https://www.etsy.com/listing/4600784009)). (L to M)
- A skilled artist builds clusters at **150–300 balloons/hour**
  ([Balloon Decoration Guide](https://balloon-decoration-guide.com/how-do-you-determine-your-rates-for-your-balloon-decorations.html)).
  At 10/ft that is 15–30 ft/h for clustering alone. Inflating, colour-sorting,
  frame work, loading and install come on top. (M)
- DIY and kit sellers quote 45–120 minutes for 6–10 ft. A first-timer needs 3–4 h
  for about 16 ft
  ([A Beautiful Mess](https://abeautifulmess.com/wprm_print/easy-balloon-garland-diy-with-video)). (L)
- **Working assumption:** 0.25–0.3 h per foot all-in for a pro, plus a fixed 1 h
  install and 0.5–1 h teardown per job.

### 2e. Travel cost

- **IRS 2026 business mileage rate: 72.5¢/mile for Jan 1–Jun 30, 2026**
  ([IRS IR-2025-128](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/irs-sets-2026-business-standard-mileage-rate-at-725-cents-per-mile-up-25-cents)).
  **It rose to 76¢/mile from July 1, 2026** under Announcement 2026-11, a rare
  mid-year change
  ([Journal of Accountancy](https://www.journalofaccountancy.com/news/2026/jul/irs-raises-standard-mileage-rates-for-remainder-of-2026/);
  [Current Federal Tax Developments](https://www.currentfederaltaxdevelopments.com/blog/2026/7/10/technical-review-of-announcement-2026-11-regarding-revised-standard-mileage-rates)). (H)
  Use **$0.76/mile** for current pricing.
- The Balloon Babe's $1.50/mile (R01) is about 2× IRS. The difference pays for
  drive *time*.

### 2f. Reported margins

- Materials typically run **10–20% of the final price**. Gross margins of
  **50–80%** are reported for decor services, and a model example uses 72.5% on
  a $775 average event
  ([FinModelsLab](https://finmodelslab.com/blogs/how-much-makes/custom-balloon-decor-service);
  [Balloon Decoration Guide, profit margins](https://www.balloon-decoration-guide.com/balloon-business-profit-margins.html)). (L: these are vendor-affiliated templates)
- **Net margin for home-based balloon businesses: 25–30%**
  ([Balloon Decoration Guide](https://www.balloon-decoration-guide.com/balloon-business-profit-margins.html)). (L)
- Watch markup versus margin: a **50% markup is only a 33% margin**
  ([Go International, UK](https://www.gointernational.co.uk/blog/?p=6634)). (M)

### 2g. Worked example A: cost per foot, organic garland (air-filled)

Assumptions: Sempertex or Tuftex at Toy World-type prices, and a mid-point for
Qualatex. Standard fullness of 10/ft in the Shimmer & Confetti mix. Labour
valued at **$45/h**, a target owner wage, not a market statistic.

| Line | Standard (10/ft) | Lush (15/ft) |
|---|---|---|
| 5" ×3.4 @ $0.075 / ×5 | $0.26 | $0.38 |
| 11" ×5.3 @ $0.25 / ×8 | $1.33 | $2.00 |
| 18" ×0.4 @ $0.45 / ×0.8 | $0.18 | $0.36 |
| +10% spares (pops) | $0.18 | $0.27 |
| Glue dots, strip, ties, frame wear | $0.25 | $0.30 |
| **Materials /ft** | **≈$2.20** | **≈$3.30** |
| Labour 0.275 h/ft (16.5 min) × $45 | $12.38 | $15.75 (0.35 h) |
| **Direct cost /ft (materials + paid labour)** | **≈$14.60** | **≈$19.05** |
| At $28/ft: materials as % of price | 8% | 12% |
| At $28/ft: left after materials and labour | $13.40 | $8.95 → so price lush at $35+ |

Foils, chrome (roughly 2× latex cost) and florals add materials. They mainly add
*time*, which is why the market charges $35–45/ft for them.

**Per-job version:** 20 ft standard garland at a Newport venue, 25 miles each
way, install plus same-day teardown.

| Line | Value |
|---|---|
| Price: 20 ft × $28 | $560 (above a $500 minimum) |
| Materials | $44 |
| Build 5.5 h + install 1 h + teardown 0.75 h + driving 2 × 1 h | 9.25 h *(corrected 2026-11-06; first printed as 10.25 h, an addition slip)* |
| Mileage: 2 round trips × 50 mi × $0.76 | $76 |
| Owner earns (price − materials − mileage) | $440 ÷ 9.25 h = **≈$48/h**, before overhead (insurance, storage, software, marketing). *Corrected; first printed as ≈$43/h* |

**The point:** at $28/ft with no separate travel or teardown fee, a
one-person coastal job pays a modest wage. The day goes to driving, not to
balloons. A teardown fee and a travel band are the levers, more than the
per-foot rate itself.

### 2h. Worked example B: "The Arrival" (vacation-rental surprise setup)

Assumed scope: a 6 ft mini garland over a mantel or window, a two-digit foil
number (air-filled on a stand), 15–20 floor and bed balloons, and a small
card. Average drive 25 miles each way, coordinated with a property manager's
door code. Collection after checkout, in line with the leave-no-trace
standard.

| Line | Value |
|---|---|
| Latex (60 garland + 20 loose) | ≈$20 |
| Foil numbers (assumption; R01 quotes $6–30 retail per foil) | ≈$12 |
| Card, ribbon, glue dots, bin bag | ≈$8 |
| **Materials** | **≈$40** |
| Build 1.25 h, coordination and admin 0.5 h, install 0.75 h, collection 0.5 h, driving 2 × 1 h | 5 h → **$225 at $45/h** |
| Mileage: 2 round trips × 50 mi × $0.76 | $76 |
| **Fully costed** | **≈$340** |

**Read:** the national $250–550 band (R01) fits. **$250 only works without a
return trip.** If collection is included, the floor is about $350, and $395–450
is comfortable. Batching collections, for example on a weekly property-manager
run, is the main way to lower the cost.

## 3. Pricing methods artists teach

1. **QBN formula:** `Selling price = (materials + labour + expenses) / (1 − (overhead% + profit%))`
   ([Balloon HQ](https://balloonhq.com/faq/deco_pricing/), via R01). (M) This is
   the industry's "first principles" method.
2. **"Triple the balloons, triple the labour."** The organic premium over classic
   was originally set this way, which matches $8–10 classic against $25–35
   organic ([Balloon HQ](https://balloonhq.com/faq/deco_pricing/)). (M)
3. **Cost sheet plus per-foot output.** Price arches by the foot, built from a
   cost sheet or an accountant's formula, with labour timed from
   balloons-per-hour
   ([Balloon Decoration Guide](https://balloon-decoration-guide.com/how-do-i-put-a-price-on-balloon-columns-arches-and-bouquets.html)). (M)
   Spreadsheet tools sold on Etsy, Gumroad and Payhip build this in: a
   minutes-per-foot input, 10% spares and a 3% card fee
   ([Etsy](https://www.etsy.com/listing/4600784009);
   [Balloon Artist Pro Guide](https://payhip.com/BalloonArtistProGuide);
   [Little Agency calculator](https://littleagencyco.gumroad.com/l/balloonartistcalculator)). (L)
4. **A per-foot floor as a minimum.** "Pricing for long-term success: a workbook
   on the $28 minimum" teaches $28/ft as the 2023 floor
   ([Gumroad](https://balloonbizmentor.gumroad.com/l/28perfoot)). (L)
5. **Separate decor from installation.** Show install as its own fee, a
   percentage or flat (20% at The Balloon Babe and at Inflate Louisville)
   ([Go International](https://www.gointernational.co.uk/blog/?p=6607)). (M)
6. **Materials as a share of price.** Keep materials at 10–20% of price, which
   amounts to a 5–10× materials multiplier (§2f). (L) **No source confirmed a
   standard "materials × 3" rule.** At balloon cost levels, ×3 would badly
   underprice the work.
7. **Value and occasion pricing.** Proposal and room packages are priced on the
   moment, not the balloon count: Light Up Rentals' +$550 for balloons,
   Paparazzi Proposals' $950. (M)
8. **Courses that teach pricing:**
   - "Balloon Pricing Made Easy", a 4-week course
     ([Balloon Decoration Guide](https://www.balloon-decoration-guide.com/balloon-decor-pricing-made-easy.html))
   - Make It Pretty's "How to Price Your Work", $69: stock, pricing and delivery
     fees ([Thinkific](https://makeitpretty.thinkific.com/courses/pricing))
   - "The Thrill Effect" community, $39/mo
     ([Skool](https://www.skool.com/balloon-thrill-society))
   - Sempertex Europe "Start Your Balloon Business Professional", 2 days, with a
     pricing module
     ([Sempertex Europe](https://www.sempertexeurope.com/en/workshops-10/start---professional---9--10112026-2))
   - Qualatex Fundamentals
     ([Qualatex Europe](https://europe.qualatex.com/en-gb/events/qualatex-fundamentals-bishops-stortford))

   (M)

## 6. Implications for Poppies' pricing

*Research input, not decisions.* Every range below is a suggestion for her to
test.

1. **Organic garland: $28/ft standard, $35/ft lush or with chrome, $42–45/ft with
   florals or foils.** $28 is the market middle (§1a). At about $2.20/ft materials
   plus 16.5 min/ft labour (§2g), the standard tier earns little after
   labour. The lush tier needs $35 to keep the same margin.
2. **Use a $500 install minimum, and raise it with distance.** For example:
   $500 within Lincoln County, $650 for Tillamook or Florence, $800+ inland.
   Seattle's $500–695 (R01) supports it, and §2g shows a single coastal job uses
   most of a day.
3. **Make teardown a separate, explicit fee: $95–150 local, more at distance.**
   The second round trip is roughly $76 of mileage and 1.5–2 h (§2g). National
   $50–200 bands (R01) assume city distances.
4. **Use a zone-based travel fee, not a pure per-mile fee.** If per-mile, use
   $1.50/mile (about 2× IRS $0.76) beyond a free radius. Zones read better for
   remote clients.
5. **Columns: $25–30 per foot of height, or about $230–350 each.** This matches
   Seattle's $230 and the Texas ladder (§1b).
6. **Arches: price organic arches as a flat product,** about **$375–550** for a
   two-person, 8–10 ft arch installed. Organic arches carry a 1.75–1.9× premium
   over classic at the same span (R01).
7. **Ceiling clouds: "from $450" for small rooms, "from $1,200–1,500" as a
   statement piece,** quoted on area and ceiling height. That is the only
   published structure found (§1c).
8. **"The Arrival": $395 base (including collection), $295 without collection
   or for property-manager batch accounts, $550+ for a premium tier.** The cost
   build-up (§2h) sets the floor at about $340 with collection. Airbnb Services
   comparables run $125–415, and Uberoom's $99.95 setup fee shows that the access
   and setup component alone is worth about $100.
9. **Proposal setups: $650–1,200, in tiers.** Portland and Seattle proposal
   packages are $495–1,450 (§1e), and balloons are worth +$550 as an add-on at
   Light Up Rentals. A "balloons + candles + sign" tier around $750, plus a
   photographer partner at $1,200+, fits that band.
10. **Grab-and-go garland: $15–18/ft (≈$120–150 for 8 ft).** This undercuts
    installed work by half, which matches the market (§1g). It is a good
    off-season and low-travel product.
11. **Storefront subscriptions: $200–275/month for a 10 ft refresh, 3-month
    minimum, set install day, skip with 7 days' notice.** These are Bluegrass's
    and Jackson County's actual terms (§1h). Group shops into one route day so
    the drive is shared.
12. **Sculptures and commissions: price as hours × a $60–75/h shop rate, plus
    materials, plus delivery, shown as a single figure.** Add a detail ladder
    (simple, detailed, gallery) on the Jonah's Twisters model, with $250 as a
    floor (R01).
13. **The Pavilion: about $3,000–4,500 all-in** for a 20×40 tent, sidewalls,
    lighting, decor and permit handling. The tent goes through at cost plus 15–25%
    (Portland tent pieces sum to about $1,300–1,550 before coast delivery; §4),
    plus a decor package of $1,200–2,000. Get a real quote from Oregon Coast
    Event Rentals before publishing anything.
14. **Rush fee: 15–20% inside 7 days, minimum $50.** Seattle uses 10%. Others
    charge 20–25% or a flat $50–75 (§1i). A solo maker's rush costs a day she
    would otherwise give to another job.
15. **Deposit: 50% non-refundable, transferable once with 30 days' notice.**
    That is the most common term found (§1i).
16. **Air-filled is the default; helium is a priced add-on of about $2–3 per
    balloon.** Gas costs about $0.35–0.45 per 9–11" balloon, more than the latex
    (§2b), and helium supply is tight in 2026.
17. **Open a trade account (e.g. Burton & Burton, Mayflower).** Retail Qualatex
    costs about 2× trade, and materials can then stay well under 15% of price
    (§2a, §2f).
18. **Check every quote against a 4–5× materials-plus-mileage test, and a
    $45–60/h effective owner rate.** Below either one, raise the price or the
    minimum. This adapts the QBN formula (§3) to a one-person studio.
19. **Wedding decor budgets average about $3,000 for florals plus decor (9% of
    $34k).** A $1,000–1,800 balloon package is a meaningful but plausible share.
    The median wedding is far smaller, so keep a sub-$700 entry package.
