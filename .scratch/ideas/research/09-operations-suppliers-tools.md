### 1.5 Account paperwork in Oregon

- **Oregon has no sales tax and issues no resale or seller's permit.** For out-of-state purchases, the Department of Revenue offers the **Oregon Business Registry Resale Certificate (form 150-800-002)**. You hand it to the seller and don't file it with the state. Sellers may accept it but aren't required to. [O1][O2] (H)
- Trade accounts that ask for a "state sales tax number" (burton+BURTON, for example) usually take an Oregon Business Registry number plus that certificate, but **expect some back-and-forth**. Several sources say an EIN and an Oregon business registration (an assumed business name or LLC with the Secretary of State) are often enough. [O3] (L–M)
- **Upside:** online wholesalers ship to Oregon with no sales tax added, so most of the "wholesale" advantage comes from case pricing, not tax.

### 2.2 Sizing, attaching and tools

- **Sizer:** collapsible box or ring sizers cost **$8–12** (2–10" or 3–14") [E5]. Pros use the **Qualatex "Very Best" Balloon Sizer** or a template sheet for 5–36" [E5][E6]. A $20 option in plywood or cardboard is easy to make for 16"+ sizes. (M)
- **Decorating strip:** 50 ft for $15–17 (Party City, Target), 25 ft for $9.99 [E7]. For garlands longer than 10 ft, pros often use **fishing line or paper tape instead of strip** (twisting duplets and quads onto line), which is cheaper and stronger. That's general trade practice, not sourced here. (M)
- **Glue dots** fill gaps and attach 5" balloons, about 2–3 dots per balloon [E8]. Buy rolls of 200–1,000 (roughly $5–15). (M)
- **Other tools**, per decorator "what's in my kit" posts [E9][E10]: balloon tying tool, scissors, cable or zip ties, paper or electrical tape, 3M Command hooks and strips (rental houses!), clear fishing line, pole-and-drape clips, a small step ladder, a tape measure, a hand pump, a cordless drill, a heat-shrink balloon cover or tarp, a microfibre cloth, Hi-Float (helium only), and a balloon shine spray (optional). (M)

### 2.3 Frames, bases and weights

- **Arch frames:** consumer kits with bases cost **$10–30** (7–10 ft, plastic) [E11]. Metal arch backdrop stands (round, square, hexagon, or 7.2 + 6 ft sets) run **$33–69** [E11]. Heavier pro **"balloon arch frame" kits** with steel tube and base plates are around **$100–250** (inference, L).
- **Mesh walls / grids:** pros use **grid wall panels** (the retail display type) or **hexagon/circle hoops** from Amazon or Faire; I didn't find a priced listing here. (L)
- **Bases and weights:** water-fillable bases cost **$49.40 per pair** and a single water-bag base **$27.98** [E11]. **Sandbags** (photo or film type, 15–25 lb) for wind cost about $10–20 each (inference). On the coast, double the usual weighting for anything outdoors. (M for need, L for price)

### 2.4 Helium (only if offered)

- Poppies is mainly air-filled, which is cheaper, lasts longer and avoids helium shortages. Helium is only needed for floating bouquets or ceiling work. (M)
- **Rental vs buy:** rental estimates run **$55–200 for 24–48 h**, and a 10–15 ft³ cylinder costs **$70–130** (content-farm estimates, L) [E12]. An institutional Airgas contract shows **$3.50 per day cylinder rent plus $52 handling or delivery** per cylinder [E13] (M; not an Oregon retail price). Welding suppliers such as **Airgas, Matheson and Praxair/Linde** rent large cylinders, often with a monthly or annual lease. **Call the Newport or Salem branch.**
- **Regulator:** many short-term rentals include a balloon regulator. A **dual regulator** is needed for foil balloons [E12]. Buy your own regulator (~$40–120, L) once helium becomes regular.
- **Recommendation:** at launch, don't hold helium. Subcontract or buy fills for the occasional bouquet. Revisit after 10 helium jobs.

## 3. Transport and handling

### 3.1 Vehicle size

- Shop rules of thumb: **6 ft garland fits a large sedan; 8 ft garlands fit an SUV with no other cargo; 10 ft garlands fit large SUVs and minivans.** One shop says "two 6 ft garlands is the most that fits in the average SUV" [H1][H2]. (M)
- Flat-floor lengths: **cargo van ~120–133 in**, minivan ~92 in, SUV ~72 in [H3]. (L, a furniture-moving source)
- **Standard practice: build in 4–6 ft sections and join on site.** For anything longer than 5 ft, build and transport in clusters, then assemble at the venue [H1]. (M) This makes vehicle length matter less than **volume**. A minivan with the seats out (Sienna or Pacifica) or a **high-roof cargo van** (Transit or ProMaster) is the upgrade path.
- **Bags:** large clear poly "balloon bags" or **mattress or "jumbo" poly bags** (king mattress bags run about $10–20 for several, inference) protect sections from wind and rain between the van and the venue. (M)

### 3.2 Heat, cold and the coast

- **Heat is the main risk.** Parked cars and trunks overheat, so run the A/C, keep sections out of direct sun, and don't over-pack: "if you need to squeeze them together, expect popping" [H4][H5]. (M)
- **Cold:** air-filled latex shrinks a little in the cold and re-expands in warmth. Don't inflate to full size in a cold garage and then move into a warm venue, or **under-inflate by about 5%** (common decorator advice, L). Helium contracts noticeably in the cold [H5].
- **Oxidation:** latex "hazes" or goes matte in UV and heat, so **inflate as late as practical** and keep finished pieces out of sunlight. (M) Hi-Shine-type sprays slow it (L).
- **Coastal humidity and rain:** wet latex looks streaky, and glue dots fail on damp surfaces. Bring towels and a tent or tarp. **Fog and rain aren't fatal to air-filled latex, but wind is.** Anchor every 2–3 ft and keep beach pieces low (garland on a frame, not freestanding arches). These are inferences from general practice plus your coastal context (M). See the companion research on beach permits and sustainability.
- **Highway 101 drives:** 45–90 min between jobs. Pack sections so they **don't touch hot windows** (use window shades), and strap or net them.

### 3.3 Storing latex stock

- Store **cool, dark and dry**, away from direct sun, **fluorescent light** and heat, and never in a vehicle or a sunny window. Keep balloons in their original bags until needed [H6][H7]. (M)
- One wholesale guide targets **50–68 °F with low humidity** [H8]. (L–M)
- Shelf life is **about 1–2 years** if stored well; old latex goes brittle and pops [H6][H8]. Rotate stock first-in, first-out. Lidded opaque bins in an interior closet work; a coastal garage that's damp and cold works less well. (M)
