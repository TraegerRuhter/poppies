# How a new balloon studio gets found and trusted: reviews, local search, social, directories, referrals

Date: 2026-11-06
Question: How do small local event and decor vendors, especially balloon artists, get found and trusted? What works best for a brand-new studio with almost no reviews?

> **Sourcing caveat.** I couldn't fetch pages directly here, so every finding comes from web-search result snippets. Each claim links to the page its snippet came from. I did not read most pages in full. Many hits were SEO-vendor blogs, competitor directories or low-quality mirror sites, and I mark those as such. Confidence: **High** means a primary source (Google, FTC, platform help pages) or several sources agree. **Med** means a credible secondary source. **Low** means a single vendor blog, an anecdote or conflicting figures. Check prices and policy wording on the live pages before spending money or quoting rules to clients.

## 1. Google Business Profile (GBP): the most important single asset

**There is a balloon category.** "Balloon artist" (internal id `balloon_artist`) and "Balloon store" both show up in third-party copies of Google's category list, which is pulled from the API ([GMB Club list](https://gmb-club.com/en/google-business-profile-categories), [RankLocal list, 6 Oct 2026](https://getranklocal.com/tools/google-business-profile-categories)). You get one primary category and up to nine more ([Daltonluka](https://daltonluka.com/blog/google-my-business-categories)). Google changes the list without announcement, so confirm in the dashboard. **High/Med.**
- Practical setup: primary **Balloon artist**. Secondary candidates to check in the dashboard: "Wedding service" (one balloon vendor uses this as its primary, per [this GBP listing](https://business.google.com/v/_/AKsrIN2BPlX25dyh9D9mkwBff66ES6tJYxG0Ggn5PburiULT4PTxvAoJh1UU/f060/_)), "Party planner", "Event planner", "Party equipment rental service" for the tent, and "Wedding decorator" if it exists. I could not confirm a party-rental category name. Only add categories that describe real services. Category stuffing tends to backfire. **Med.**
- One guide suggests "Balloon artist" or "Party decor" for a balloon decor company ([Balloon Suite guide](https://www.balloonsuite.com/how-to-create-a-google-business-profile-for-your-balloon-decor-company)). **Low/Med.**

**Service-area business (SAB) setup.** Poppies doesn't serve customers at an address, so hide the address and list service areas by city or postcode. Google says you can no longer set a radius ([Google help, mirrored copy](https://dcmpx.remotevs.com/com/google/support/SL/business/answer/3038163)). One balloon business blog claims to use a 50-mile radius ([The Bright Balloon](https://www.podpage.com/the-bright-balloon-1/blog/put-your-business-on-the-map-literally)), which is likely an older setup. A SAB may have only one profile per metro area. **High.**
- For Poppies: list the coast towns actually served (e.g. Lincoln City, Depoe Bay, Newport, Waldport, Yachats, Pacific City, Tillamook, Florence, whichever apply). Ranking still leans heavily on proximity to the hidden address, so expect to show in the local pack near home base and rely on the website and directories farther out. **Med** (general local-SEO consensus, not verified this session).

**Photos.** The BrightLocal GMB Insights study (c. 2018-19, 45,000 businesses) found that profiles with 100+ photos averaged far more calls, direction requests and website clicks. The headline figures are +520% calls, +2,717% direction requests and +1,065% website clicks versus the average ([Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/new-research-shows-strong-link-between-google-my-business-photo-quantity-and-search-performance-320199/)). The study is **correlational**, and "2026" versions on aggregator sites recycle the old numbers. A steady photo cadence (roughly 1-2 a week) is common practitioner advice. Avoid stock images ([Siteswan](https://siteswan.com/how-to-set-up-optimize-your-google-business-profile-step-by-step-guide)). **Med.**
- Balloon work is very photogenic, so this is a cheap advantage. Upload every install from several angles, plus process shots and the tent.

**Posts.** I found no solid primary data showing Posts move rankings. One secondary blog attributes "+35% direction requests" to weekly posters but could not tie it to BrightLocal ([Pipeline Marketing](https://pipeline-marketing.pages.dev/blog/gbp-posts-do-they-work/)). Treat Posts as cheap freshness and conversion content (seasonal offers, "booking spring weddings"), not a ranking lever. **Low.**

**Q&A is gone.** Google shut the Q&A API on 3 Nov 2025 and replaced public Q&A with an AI "Ask" button. Its answers draw on the profile, the reviews and the website ([Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-maps-qa-feature-ask-40594.html), [BrightLocal](https://www.brightlocal.com/learn/google-business-profile-qa)). **High.** So the services list, business description, website FAQ and review text are now what the AI uses to answer "do they do weddings?" or "do they deliver to Yachats?". Put that information in plain words on the website.

**Reviews and ranking.** Whitespark's Darren Shaw says review recency got much more weight in 2025. He calls it one of the most underrated factors and would put it in his top five, up from #20 in the 2023 survey ([Whitespark Local Insider](https://insider.whitespark.ca/p/the-most-underrated-local-ranking-factor-in-2025-e544)). A secondary summary of the 2026 Whitespark report gives local-pack weightings of GBP 32%, reviews 20%, on-page 15% and behavioural 9% ([Digital Thrive summary](https://www.digitalthriveai.com/en-us/resources/seo/google-updates-local-ranking-documentation/)). It is unverified, so check the [Whitespark report](https://whitespark.ca/local-search-ranking-factors). **Med.**
- **Implication:** a **steady trickle** of reviews (one or two per event, every month) beats a launch-week blitz.
- **Velocity can backfire.** Google filters reviews, can pause new reviews on a profile for 7-30 days and can unpublish existing ones when it sees suspicious spikes ([Search Engine Roundtable on spike emails](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-business-profiles-spike-in-spam-reviews-42123.html), [DEV analysis, Low](https://dev.to/erin_shepard_index1/the-q4-shift-inside-google-new-review-moderation-standards-e99)). Reviews from accounts with few prior reviews reportedly get filtered more often. Google blocked 240M fake reviews in 2024 ([SER](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-maps-spam-fighting-2024-39186.html)). **Med.** Don't ask 20 friends to post on day one.

**Review link and QR code.** Google now generates an official review link and QR code. In the profile, go to Read reviews, then Get more reviews, then Share. The QR code is available on desktop only ([Google help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/16816815?hl=en), [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-offers-qr-codes-to-get-reviews-from-customers-452966)). **High.**

**Measurement on GBP.** Add UTM parameters to the website link (e.g. `?utm_source=google&utm_medium=organic&utm_campaign=gbp`) so profile clicks show up separately in analytics ([BrightLocal](https://www.brightlocal.com/learn/google-business-profile-utm-tracking/), [Search Engine Land guide](https://searchengineland.com/guide/utms-for-google-business-profile)). Don't treat Performance impressions as a success metric. **High/Med.**

## 2. Compliant review generation

**What the law and policies allow:** you can ask **every** customer for an honest review, with no incentive and no steering on rating or content.

- **Google:** "Fake engagement" includes "discourage or prohibit negative reviews, or selectively solicit positive reviews". It is also prohibited to offer incentives (payment, discounts, free goods or services) for posting, revising or removing a review. Asking for genuine reviews without incentives or influence is allowed ([Google contribution policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114)). An April 2026 update reportedly bars requiring staff to hit review quotas or solicit specific content ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/google-tightens-maps-review-policy-staff-names-and-quotas-now-banned/)). **High** for the core policy, **Med** for the 2026 additions.
- **US FTC Consumer Review Rule (16 CFR Part 465)**, announced 14 Aug 2024, effective 21 Oct 2024. It bans fake or AI-generated reviews and buying them, **incentives conditioned on sentiment** (positive or negative), undisclosed insider reviews (owners, employees and **relatives**), review suppression through threats or intimidation, and presenting a company-controlled review site as independent. Civil penalties run to roughly $51,744 per violation, inflation-adjusted ([FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials), [Davis Wright Tremaine](https://dwt.com/insights/2024/08/ftc-finalizes-rule-banning-fake-consumer-reviews), [Goodwin](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/blogs/2024/08/ftc-publishes-new-rule-banning-fake-reviews-and-testimonials)). The FTC sent warning letters in late 2025, which signals enforcement ([Venable](https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2025/12/ftc-signals-heightened-enforcement-of-new)). **High.**
  - Note the gap between the two. The FTC bans incentives **conditioned on sentiment**. Google bans **any** incentive for a review. Follow the stricter one, Google's: no incentives at all.
- **Review gating is out.** Sending happy customers to Google and unhappy ones to a private form counts as "selectively soliciting". Ask everyone the same way ([Google policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114)). **High.**
- **Yelp:** Yelp's guidance is **don't ask for Yelp reviews at all**, including through mailing lists or third-party tools. Systematic solicitation can bring a search-ranking penalty or a consumer alert ([Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/yelp-increasingly-cracking-review-solicitation-across-internet-286290), [BrightLocal guidelines](https://www.brightlocal.com/learn/review-management/dos-and-donts/review-platform-guidelines/)). **High.**

**Timing and mechanics that event vendors use**
- **Response rates:** WeddingPro reports that about 1 in 3 clients will review if reminded at least once. Easy Weddings (AU) reports about 20% from a single ask and 33%+ with one follow-up ([WeddingPro](https://pros.weddingpro.com/blog/want-more-reviews-start-doing-these-5-things/), [Easy Weddings](https://www.easyweddings.com.au/pro-education/how-to-ask-for-customer-reviews)). Secondary summaries of BrightLocal's 2025 survey claim 76-83% of consumers asked go on to review. The figures conflict ([Search Lab Digital](https://searchlabdigital.com/blog/data-from-brightlocals-consumer-review-survey/)). **Med/Low.**
- **Timing advice conflicts.** Some say 24-48 h, while excitement is high (photo-booth guide, [TrueReview](https://truereview.co/post/how-photo-booth-and-event-rental-businesses-get-more-google-reviews)). Others say 7-14 days ([Eventnest](https://eventnest.ph/blog/how-to-ask-wedding-clients-for-reviews-templates-included/)) or 2-3 weeks after a wedding ([WeddingPro](https://pros.weddingpro.com/blog/want-more-reviews-start-doing-these-5-things/)). **Low/Med.**
  - **For balloons:** decor peaks the day it goes up, and clients see it at once. Sensible pattern for Poppies:
    - (a) A soft verbal ask plus a printed QR card at the reveal or teardown.
    - (b) Within 24-48 h, a "your photos" email or text with a gallery of pro shots of *their* install and the review link.
    - (c) One reminder around day 7-10.
    - (d) For weddings, a second nudge once the couple has their photographer's gallery, which is a natural moment to remember the decor.
- **Photo delivery as the review moment.** Clients want the pictures, and that is a reason to email them that isn't "please review us". Making the photos conditional on a review would be an incentive, so it is banned. Deliver the photos regardless, and put the ask underneath.
- **Make it easy:** a direct review link, one tap. Something like "A sentence about what you'd tell a friend helps other families find us" is fine. Don't suggest a star rating or script the wording. Don't stop people photographing the install to put in their own review. Reviews with photos help.
- **Reply to every review**, good and bad, briefly and graciously. The FTC rule bans threatening or intimidating reviewers. **High.**

## 5. Social content that works for balloon artists

**Formats** (practitioner idea lists, no hard performance data found; **Low/Med**):
- **Time-lapse build**, from empty frame to finished arch. This is the most-cited format ([Socialmon, 68 balloon post ideas](https://www.socialmon.ai/blog/68-balloon-decor-business-instagram-post-ideas-with-examples)). Time-lapse and "evolution" content works across art niches ([Artfinder](https://www.artfinder.com/blog/post/reels-ten-tips-guide-april-2024/)).
- **Before/after of the venue**: an empty room or beach, then the reveal. Ideal for the 20×40 tent.
- **Process and ASMR**: the inflator hum, the squeak of tying, a garland being fluffed, the "pop" of a strip coming together. Balloon popping videos get tens of millions of views on YouTube ([vidIQ channel stats](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/UC5TaF7HJmTsN396b9Aq_VCg), [Refinery29](https://refinery29.com/en-au/2022/12/11246683/tiktok-memorable-moments-2022)), but that is entertainment, not buyers. Use ASMR as texture, not the main strategy. **Low.**
- **Pricing explainers**: why garlands cost what they do. Labour scales with length, organic styles can use up to 4× the product, foils cost 2-3× latex, plus delivery and venue insurance ([Shimmer & Confetti](https://shimmerandconfetti.ca/blogs/the-shimmer-and-confetti-blog/price-of-balloon-arches-garlands-and-accessories), [ThePricer](https://www.thepricer.org/balloon-arch-cost/)). Typical public price points are $10-20 per linear foot for garland. These posts pre-qualify leads and justify premium pricing. **Med.**
- **Guests posing with the install**, plus a gentle branded hashtag ([Socialmon](https://www.socialmon.ai/blog/68-balloon-decor-business-instagram-post-ideas-with-examples)).
- **Seasonal "booking now" posts** (Mother's Day, graduation, holidays).

**Platform mechanics (2025-26)**
- **Instagram caps hashtags at 5 per post or Reel** as of Dec 2025. Mosseri says hashtags help search but don't boost reach, and that caption keywords and on-screen text matter more ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/news/story/no-more-hashtag-stuffing-instagram-limits-hashtag-usage-to-just-5-per-reel-and-post-507444-2025-12-19)). **High.** Use 3-5 specific tags (#oregoncoastwedding, #lincolncityoregon, #balloongarland) and **write the town and venue names in the caption and on screen**.
- **Collab posts** put one post on both accounts' grids and feeds, and work for feed posts and Reels. Vendors claim around 2.5× reach or +48% engagement, unverified ([Later](https://later.com/blog/instagram-collabs/), [Metricool](https://metricool.com/collaborative-reels-instagram/)). Collaborator limits are reported inconsistently (1 vs 3). **Med.** Collab-post every install with the venue, the photographer and the planner.
- **Location tags** on the venue and town. Tag every vendor. Venues and photographers often reshare work that credits them.
- **Cadence:** I found no balloon-specific evidence. A sustainable 3-4 Reels plus a few stories per week, repurposing formats that worked before, is common creator advice ([Katie Stack](https://katiestack.substack.com/p/what-ive-noticed-about-reels)). **Low.**
- **Pinterest for weddings:** Pinterest publishes a yearly wedding trends report ([Social Media Today, 2026 report](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/pinterest-releases-2026-wedding-trends-report/818774/), [Style Me Pretty roundup](https://perspectives.stylemepretty.com/pinterest-predicts-2026-the-complete-wedding-trend-roundup/)). I found no current balloon-specific Pinterest stat. Earlier wedding press called large balloon installations a trend ([MN Bride](https://mnbride.com/now-trending-massive-balloon-installations-make-unforgettable-decor)). Pins are evergreen and link straight to the website, which suits a portfolio-led brand. **Med.**

## 6. Building a portfolio from zero

- **Styled shoots.** Several vendors collaborate on a mock wedding in exchange for images. Plan at least a month ahead and on weekdays, because weekends are wedding days. Choose the target publication *before* the shoot, since each has its own requirements and exclusivity terms. Submit to one blog at a time, often through Two Bright Lights. Send about 75% detail shots and 25% portraits, in both orientations, and credit every vendor ([Junebug](https://junebugweddings.com/wedding-photo-blog/photobug/5-tips-for-creating-styled-shoots-for-publication/), [Nashville Bride Guide](https://nashvillebrideguide.com/how-to-get-published-on-a-wedding-blog/), [Green Wedding Shoes](https://greenweddingshoes.com/how-to-put-together-a-styled-editorial-for-a-blog-feature/), [Loverly](https://loverly.com/planning/wedding-101/power-of-styled-shoots-for-wedding-vendors)). **Med/High.** Balloon decor gives a styled shoot an unusual hero element, which helps it get picked up.
- **Photographer collaborations.** Photographers need fresh backdrops for portfolios and minis. Offer an install for a portrait day in exchange for full-resolution images with credit.
- **Showcase or discounted installs** at venues, open houses, chamber events, a café or shop window, or a community festival. Use these for **photos and relationships, not reviews**. Under Google's policy, asking for a review in exchange for a free install would count as an incentive.
- **Vendor networking.** Qualatex (a balloon manufacturer) advises aiming to be a venue's *preferred balloon vendor* and keeping venues updated on designs that suit their spaces ([Qualatex](https://africa.qualatex.com/en-gb/news/4-tips-wedding-business-bliss)). High-end positioning means acting as a reliable creative partner and offering maintenance and cleanup ([The Bright Balloon](https://www.podpage.com/the-bright-balloon-1/blog/attracting-high-end-balloon-clients), [luxury market post](https://www.podpage.com/the-bright-balloon-1/blog/breaking-into-the-luxury-event-market)). **Med.**

## 7. Referral and partner programs; website trust formats

- **Venue preferred-vendor lists** are the strongest channel for wedding decor. Coast venues (e.g. Chinook Winds, Pelican Brewing Siletz Bay and Cannon Beach, Surfsand Resort, Abernethy Center, Agate Beach Inn) appear on Zola and WeddingWire, and many say they welcome outside decor ([Zola Lincoln City](https://zola.com/wedding-vendors/search/lincoln-city-or), [Zola Cannon Beach](https://zola.com/wedding-vendors/search/cannon-beach-or), [WeddingWire Newport](https://www.weddingwire.com/c/or-oregon/newport/wedding-venues/11-vendors.html)). I found no public preferred-vendor lists, so you have to ask each venue directly. Venues commonly require **certificates of insurance** ([ThePricer](https://www.thepricer.org/balloon-arch-cost/)). Have a COI and a one-page venue sheet ready. **Med.**
- **Planners and photographers:** referral fees between vendors are allowed and common. If a partner *recommends* Poppies publicly and gets paid for it, the FTC endorsement principles suggest they should disclose that. That is an inference from the rule's material-connection logic. **Low/Med.**
- **Website trust formats** (practitioner consensus; the AI "Ask" button and AI search also read the site):
  - Case-study pages per install: client type, venue, palette, size, setup time, a photo set, a client quote with permission, and credits for the photographer and venue.
  - A "How it works" section: inquiry, then design call, then proposal, then deposit, then install, then teardown.
  - A starting-price guide.
  - An FAQ covering service area, weather and wind on the coast, setup windows, latex and biodegradability, and venue rules.
  - Testimonials must be real, unedited in meaning, and not from undisclosed insiders ([FTC rule](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials)). **High.**

## 8. Measuring it cheaply

- A **"How did you hear about us?"** dropdown on the Astro inquiry form (required), plus a hidden field that captures `utm_source` and the referrer. This is the single best measurement tool at zero cost.
- **UTM-tag** every link: GBP website link (`utm_campaign=gbp`), Instagram bio, Nextdoor, Zola, Knot, QR cards (`utm_source=qr&utm_campaign=install-card`) ([BrightLocal](https://www.brightlocal.com/learn/google-business-profile-utm-tracking/)).
- **GBP Performance** monthly: searches by keyword, calls, website clicks. Ignore raw impressions.
- **A spreadsheet**, one row per inquiry: source, event type, date, quoted, booked (Y/N), value. After 6 months, cost per booking per channel tells you where to spend.
- **Review count and rating per platform**, tracked monthly. Target: 1-2 new Google reviews a month, steady.
- **Instagram:** saves, shares and profile visits matter more than likes. Track DMs that become inquiries.
- Optionally, a separate **Google Voice number** on the GBP to count calls coming from Google.

## Rules not to break

1. **No incentives for reviews of any kind** on Google: no discount, freebie, raffle entry or "free balloon refresh for a review". The FTC's narrower ban covers sentiment-conditioned incentives. Google's covers all of them ([Google](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114), [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials)).
2. **No review gating.** Don't send unhappy clients to a private form and happy ones to Google. Ask everyone the same way.
3. **No fake, AI-written or ghost-written reviews**, and none bought from anyone. Don't write reviews for clients "to save them time". Suggesting the content of a review is also out under the 2026 Google update.
4. **No undisclosed insider reviews.** Owners, family, employees, and arguably close friends acting as a favour must not post as ordinary customers ([BCLP on insider reviews](https://bclplaw.com/print/v2/content/1547581/part-4-what-the-ftcs-final-rule-means-for-insider-reviews.pdf)).
5. **No review suppression.** No threats, legal intimidation or "non-disparagement" clauses in contracts aimed at reviews. Federal law also voids those clauses under the Consumer Review Fairness Act (from general knowledge, not checked this session).
6. **Don't ask for Yelp reviews** at all, Yelp's rule ([SEL](https://searchengineland.com/yelp-increasingly-cracking-review-solicitation-across-internet-286290)).
7. **No review spikes.** Don't run a launch-day blitz of friends' reviews or ask people to repost removed reviews. It risks filtering or a review pause ([SER](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-business-profiles-spike-in-spam-reviews-42123.html)).
8. **One GBP only, with honest data.** No fake addresses or virtual offices, and no keyword stuffing in the business name ("Poppies Balloon Arches Lincoln City" violates the guidelines unless that's the legal name).
9. **Testimonials on the website must be real and representative.** Don't imply they're independent if you curated them, and don't edit their meaning.
10. **Disclose paid partnerships** in social collabs if money or free product changes hands.

## Ideas this suggests for Poppies

Effort: **S** = an afternoon, **M** = a few days or ongoing light work, **L** = a project.

**Foundations**
1. **Set up GBP as an SAB with primary category "Balloon artist"**, coast towns listed, address hidden. *Why:* the "balloon decor near me" local pack is the biggest free intent channel. **S**
2. **Seed GBP with 30+ real photos, then add 2-3 a week.** Include process shots, the tent, and the van and gear. *Why:* photo count correlates strongly with calls and clicks. **S then M**
3. **Write an "AI-answerable" FAQ** on the site and in the GBP description: service towns, weddings yes, tent, wind and weather, lead time, starting prices. *Why:* Q&A is gone, and the "Ask" button and AI search answer from this text. **S**
4. **Add "How did you hear?" and UTM capture to the inquiry form.** *Why:* free attribution from day one. **S**
5. **Claim free listings:** Zola, Knot, WeddingWire, Nextdoor, Yelp, Apple Business Connect, Bing Places, GigSalad free. Don't pay for any of them yet. *Why:* citations and coverage at no cost. **S**
6. **UTM-tag every outbound link and print QR.** *Why:* tells you which channel books jobs. **S**

**Reviews (compliant)**
7. **A "reveal card"**: a small letterpress card left at every install with a Google review QR code and "Tell a friend what you thought". It fits the gallery-wall brand. *Why:* an ask at peak emotion that stays on brand. **S**
8. **A "Your Poppies photos" email within 24-48 h**: 6-10 edited shots of their install, free to download, with the review link underneath and one reminder at day 7-10. *Why:* the photos give a reason to get in touch, and the review ask is a gentle add-on. **M**
9. **A second nudge for weddings** after the photographer's gallery arrives, asking for a review on Knot or Zola as well as Google. *Why:* builds toward the 7-review Best of Weddings threshold. **S**
10. **Reply to every review within 48 h** in a warm, brief house voice. *Why:* shows care to future readers and keeps the profile active. **S**
11. **Ask every client, the same way, every time.** Put it in the job checklist. *Why:* that is the compliant way, and it produces the steady trickle that recency rewards. **S**

**Content**
12. **A signature Reel format: "Empty → Poppies"** time-lapse, 15-20 s, same framing and sound every time. *Why:* repeatable and recognisable, and the best-cited balloon format. **M**
13. **"Coast vs. balloons" series**: building in wind, salt air, beach permits, how the tent saves the day. *Why:* specific to the coast, it builds trust and gets found in search. **M**
14. **A calm pricing explainer series** ("What $400 of garland looks like", "Why organic garlands take 4× the balloons"). *Why:* pre-qualifies leads and sets a premium anchor. **M**
15. **Quiet "balloon ASMR" B-roll** as Stories: tying, fluffing, the inflator, no music. *Why:* cheap to film and fits the restrained aesthetic. Don't make it the core strategy. **S**
16. **Collab-post every install** with the venue and the photographer, and put town and venue names in the caption and on screen, plus 3-5 hashtags max. *Why:* doubles reach and fits the 2025 hashtag cap. **S**
17. **Pinterest boards by palette and venue type** ("Coastal neutrals", "Beach tent wedding") linking to case-study pages. *Why:* evergreen wedding planning traffic to the site. **M**

**Portfolio from zero**
18. **One styled shoot this winter** with a coast photographer, planner, florist and venue, pitched in advance to Junebug, Oregon Bride or *Our Coast Weddings*. *Why:* off-season, a publishable portfolio, and four new vendor relationships. **L**
19. **"Portrait-day backdrop" offer to photographers**: a free install for their mini-session day in exchange for images and tags. *Why:* photographers become a referral network. **M**
20. **One public "gallery" install**: a shop window, café, library or gallery in Lincoln City or Newport, credited with a small placard and QR code to the site, not a review ask. *Why:* it shows the gallery-wall positioning in a public place. **M**
21. **A seasonal community install** (festival, Fourth of July, holiday lights) for a local nonprofit. *Why:* goodwill, press and photos. Don't make reviews part of the deal. **M**

**Partners and referrals**
22. **A venue kit**: a one-page PDF per venue with what fits their space, setup and teardown windows, the COI, and a starting price. Deliver it in person. *Why:* this is how you get onto preferred-vendor lists. **M**
23. **Bring the COI and a no-helium or low-mess policy to every venue conversation.** *Why:* venues care about risk, and this answers their objections. **S**
24. **A "vendor thank-you" referral program**: a flat thank-you fee or a gift to planners and photographers who refer a booked job, documented, with disclosure if they recommend Poppies publicly. *Why:* it rewards the people actually sending wedding work. **S**
25. **Host the first "Coast Wedding Pros" mixer** in the 20×40 tent with a balloon installation. Invite venues, photographers, planners and florists. *Why:* the coast has no current wedding show, so Poppies becomes the connector. Bold. **L**
26. **A "Coast Wedding Walk" mini bridal showcase** co-hosted with one venue in late winter. *Why:* there is no coast bridal show, only Portland, Eugene and Bend. Bold. **L**
27. **Answer "who does balloons?" in local Facebook groups** as a person, not with ads, and follow each group's promo-day rules. *Why:* small-town word of mouth runs through these groups. **S**
28. **Nextdoor Faves link** on the reveal card's back and in the follow-up email, as a second option. Aim for the 2027 Fave Awards. *Why:* free neighbourhood trust signal for birthday and celebration work. **S**

**Website trust**
29. **Case-study pages per install**: the brief, palette, venue, size, build time, a client quote with permission, and credited vendors. *Why:* replaces review volume while reviews are few, and feeds AI answers. **M**
30. **A "How it works" page in 5 steps** with real photos of each step and an honest "what we need from you". *Why:* lowers inquiry anxiety without a booking calendar. **S**
31. **A transparent "Starting at" pricing page** (per-foot garland, arch, tent packages). *Why:* filters out tyre-kickers and matches the pricing-explainer content. **S**
32. **Embed real Google reviews** on the site, all of them or the most recent N rather than hand-picked ones, with a link to the full profile. *Why:* social proof that stays within the FTC rule's representativeness expectations. **S**

**Bold or experimental**
33. **A "Poppies Studio Wall"**: a rotating monthly balloon art piece in a partner venue or café, numbered like gallery editions ("No. 07"), with each new one posted. *Why:* turns the brand concept into recurring content and local talk. **M**
34. **Month-long tests capped at about $50**: Thumbtack and GigSalad free tier only. Track bookings and drop them if nothing comes. *Why:* cheap test of whether marketplace demand exists on the coast. **S**
35. **Skip paid Knot/WeddingWire in year 1** and revisit once there are 7+ wedding reviews. *Why:* reported lead quality is poor, 12-month contracts are a lock-in, and balloons are rarely searched for first. **S** (a decision)
36. **A quarterly "local pack audit"**: search "balloon decor" from each coast town on a phone and screenshot it. *Why:* free rank tracking that shows where GBP stops reaching and where to focus content. **S**
