# Oregon business setup for Poppies: legal, tax, insurance, permits, costs

Date: 2026-11-07
Status: research note (complete first pass, with gaps marked)

> **This is general information, not legal, tax or insurance advice.** It is a starting map for conversations with the Oregon Coast SBDC, an Oregon business lawyer, a CPA/EA and an insurance broker. Fees and thresholds change; confirm each number at the source before you pay or sign anything.

**Sourcing caveat.** Everything here comes from web-search result snippets. Fetching pages directly was blocked in this session: `www.newportoregon.gov` failed to resolve, and the other government sites were not retried after that. Earlier Poppies research found that sos.oregon.gov and USPTO were also unreachable. So no figure below was read off a live official page. Where the search snippet came from an official domain (oregon.gov, irs.gov, city sites), confidence is higher. Where it came from filing services, insurer marketing pages or blogs, it is lower. Confidence tags: **H** = official source, consistent across snippets. **M** = official source but dated or partly conflicting, or several consistent secondary sources. **L** = single or secondary source, conflicting figures, or my own inference.

**Bottom line.** For a one-or-two-person studio, the legal setup is cheap. Expect about **$100–$300 at the state level** (plus a $100/yr LLC annual report if you form an LLC), and **about $100–$200 per city per year** for each coast city you work in. The real recurring costs are **liability insurance (about $200–$600/yr base, more with tent, equipment and auto coverage)** and, if business driving isn't covered, **commercial auto ($600–$2,500/yr)**. The two things most likely to catch Poppies out:
1. **City business licences in each city where you work.** Several Lincoln County cities license out-of-town businesses that work inside city limits.
2. **The 20×40 tent.** At 800 sq ft it is over both Oregon Fire Code permit thresholds (400 sq ft enclosed, 700 sq ft open-sided). It almost always needs a fire permit and an NFPA 701 flame-resistance certificate. On the beach it also triggers an OPRD special use permit.

## 1. Business structure: sole proprietorship vs LLC

| Item | Sole proprietorship | Single-member LLC | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| State formation filing | None. You exist as soon as you trade. | Articles of Organization with Oregon SOS Corporation Division: **$100**, non-refundable | M (consistent across filing-service sites; not read on SOS fee page) |
| Annual upkeep | None at state level (except ABN renewal) | Annual report due on the LLC's anniversary: **$100/yr** | M |
| Trading as "Poppies" | Must register an **Assumed Business Name**: $50 | Must register an ABN too, unless the LLC's legal name is exactly "Poppies" (e.g. "Poppies LLC" can trade as "Poppies LLC" without one) | H/M |
| Federal tax | Schedule C + Schedule SE on your 1040 | Same by default (disregarded entity); can elect S-corp later | H (IRS) |
| Liability shield | None. Personal assets are exposed. | Separates business debts and claims from personal assets, if you keep finances separate. **It does not replace insurance.** | M (general principle) |
| Optional | n/a | Name reservation ~$100 (rarely needed) | L |

**Assumed business name (ABN).**
- **Fee and term.** The SOS License Directory lists the ABN at **$50, a 2-year term, renewed every 2 years** (renewal also $50 per secondary sources). Confidence: H for fee and term.
- **Renewal timing.** One statute summary puts the renewal window at the 30 days before each second anniversary (ORS 648.017). If you miss it, the registration can be administratively cancelled. Confidence: M.
- **When it is required.** It is triggered when the name you trade under does not conspicuously disclose every owner's "real and true name" (ORS 648.005/648.007). It is filed once, with the Secretary of State, and covers all counties (ORS 648.010). For an LLC the comparison point is its filed name. Adding words (even "& Co.") makes it an assumed name. Confidence: M (statute via secondary summaries).
- **Penalties.** An unregistered user can't maintain a lawsuit in Oregon courts for the business until it registers (curable), plus a civil penalty up to $100 (ORS 648.990). Confidence: M.
- **The ABN is not trademark protection.** Registering "Poppies" as an ABN does nothing about the USPTO POPPIE mark (serial 98162236) or other Oregon "Poppies" event businesses. See `.scratch/naming/poppies-name-collision.md`. Confidence: H (general principle).

**Other state-level items (from general knowledge, not searched this session, L):**
- Oregon has no general state business licence. Specialty licences come from boards (SOS "check state license requirements" page; H that there is no general licence).
- An EIN from the IRS is free. A sole proprietor with no employees can use their SSN, but a free EIN keeps the SSN off W-9s and invoices.
- Hiring any employee triggers Oregon workers' compensation and payroll registration. Confirm with SBDC before hiring a helper. Paying a friend cash "to help with the arch" is the classic trap.

**Practical approach.** Hold a licence for your home-base city from day one. For every other city, call or email city hall *before the first paid job there* with one question: "Do you require a licence for an out-of-town decor vendor doing a one-day install inside city limits?" Some cities have temporary or event-vendor categories (Depoe Bay's $50 "transient" tier is one example). If you work in 3–4 cities a year, **budget $400–$700/yr** for city licences in total (L: my arithmetic from the fees above).

## 3. Taxes

**No sales tax.** Oregon imposes no general sales, use or transaction tax. You don't collect tax from customers and you can't get a "sales tax permit" (Oregon DOR Sales Tax page; H).

**Buying wholesale (balloons, helium, frames).**
- **Wholesale accounts are not about tax.** Distributors want proof that you are a real business so they can give you trade pricing. That usually means an SOS registration or ABN, sometimes an EIN.
- **Oregon resale certificate.** For out-of-state suppliers that would otherwise charge their state's tax, Oregon provides the **Oregon Business Registry Resale Certificate (Form 150-800-002)**. It shows the seller you are registered in Oregon and buying for resale. **Sellers are not required to accept it.** Some suppliers accept the Multistate Tax Commission uniform certificate instead. (Oregon DOR; H for the form's existence, M for how suppliers treat it.)
- **Supplies vs resale.** Whether balloons used in an installation count as "resale" or as "supplies consumed in a service" can matter in the supplier's state. Ask the accountant. (L)
- **Shipping into Oregon.** Many online suppliers simply don't charge tax when shipping to an Oregon address, because there is no Oregon tax to collect. (M, general)

**Oregon personal income tax.**
- **Rates.** Business profit (sole prop or single-member LLC) flows to your OR-40. Rates are **4.75% / 6.75% / 8.75% / 9.9%**. Rates: H/M. Bracket cutoffs: sources conflicted; the 8.75% band appears to run up to $125k single. L for exact thresholds; check DOR.
- **Estimated payments.** Required in most cases if you expect **$1,000 or more** of Oregon tax after credits and withholding. Due Apr 15, Jun 15, Sep 15, Jan 15. Safe harbours: 90% of this year's tax or 100% of last year's. (Oregon DOR Pub. OR-ESTIMATE and OR-40 instructions; H)
- **Self-employment tax is a federal tax.** It is not part of the Oregon estimate calculation (DOR; H).

**Federal (IRS).**
- **Self-employment tax** is **15.3%** (Social Security 12.4% + Medicare 2.9%) on **92.35% of net SE earnings**. It applies once net SE earnings reach **$400**. You deduct half of it from income. (IRS Topic 554, Schedule SE; H)
- **Quarterly estimates (Form 1040-ES)** are generally required if you expect to owe **$1,000+** including SE tax (IRS Pub. 334; H).
- Rule of thumb for a first year: set aside **25–30% of profit** for federal + Oregon + SE tax. (L, common practice; ask the accountant)

**Corporate Activity Tax (CAT).** CAT is irrelevant at Poppies' scale:
- At or under **$750,000** of Oregon commercial activity, a business is **excluded from all CAT requirements**.
- Registration is due within 30 days of passing $750k.
- A return and payment are required only above **$1 million**: $250 plus 0.57% of taxable activity over $1M. (Oregon DOR; H)
- Despite the name, CAT applies to sole props and LLCs too.

**Transient lodging taxes: not Poppies' tax.**
- Lodging taxes are collected by whoever collects payment for the *stay* (DOR). The state rate is 1.5% for stays ending by Dec 31, 2026, and **2.75% from Jan 1, 2027**. Lincoln County and Lincoln City add their own (county ~10–12%; 2023 vote result not confirmed).
- A decor service at a vacation rental is not lodging. **Don't add lodging tax to your invoices.** (H that the provider collects; L that it could never apply to Poppies.)
- **One edge case:** if Poppies ever bundles a stay into a package (e.g. "proposal package incl. one night"), revisit this.

**Other local taxes.** None found for Lincoln County. Lincoln City's licence is technically an "occupation tax". Portland/Multnomah-style business income taxes don't apply on the coast. (M)

## 4. Insurance

**What venues and rental hosts will ask for:**
- **General liability (GL)** at **$1M per occurrence / $2M aggregate**. FLIP's standard is a $2M aggregate. COAI's member policy is $1M / $5M. (M)
- A **certificate of insurance (COI)** naming the venue or property owner as **additional insured**.
- Some will want this weeks before the event.

**Points to check with any insurer:**
- **Classification matters.** "Balloon twister/entertainer" programs are cheap but may not cover *installation* work: ladders, attaching to buildings, heavy frames, tents.
- **Get a quote as "event decor installer"** and disclose the tent. Ask specifically whether **tent collapse and wind-blown structures** are covered or excluded. This is the biggest coast-specific exposure. (L: inference)
- **Additional insured.** Confirm unlimited AI endorsements and same-day COI downloads. FLIP and Thimble advertise this. (M)
- **Equipment (inland marine / tools & equipment floater).** Covers the tent, frames, inflators and helium tanks in transit and at venues. GL does not cover your own gear. FLIP sells it as an add-on. **No price found** (gap). Expect a small add-on of roughly $100–$300/yr for a few thousand dollars of gear (L, unsourced estimate; get a quote).
- **Auto.** Personal auto policies typically contain a **business-use exclusion**. A claim while hauling materials or driving between job sites may be denied. Options:
  - a business-use endorsement on the personal policy (cheapest, if use is limited), or
  - **commercial auto at roughly $600–$2,500 per vehicle per year**.

  Tell your personal insurer exactly what you haul (helium cylinders especially). (M, Insureon/NerdWallet)
- **Hired help.** Workers' comp becomes mandatory with employees (see §1).
- **Professional liability / E&O** is usually unnecessary for decor. Skip unless a contract requires it. (L)

**Budget:**
- **Year one, lean:** ~$200–$600 GL + ~$100–$300 equipment + auto endorsement = **~$400–$1,000/yr**.
- **With commercial auto:** ~$1,000–$3,000/yr. (L, assembled from the ranges above)

## 5. Tents and permits

**Oregon Fire Code thresholds:**
- **Permit required:** tents over **400 sq ft** that are not open on all sides; tents over **700 sq ft** that are open on all sides. Source: Albany FD form, citing OFC 105.6.47 and ch. 31. (H)
- **Poppies' 20×40 = 800 sq ft. It needs a permit either way**, even with all sides open. (H: arithmetic on H threshold)
- The open-sided exemption up to 700 sq ft also has aggregate limits and a **12 ft clearance** to structures and other tents (model code text). (M)
- Local fire authorities can amend the thresholds (Eugene 1,500 sq ft; Gresham 200 sq ft). **The local fire marshal's rule controls.** (M)

**Flame resistance:**
- Before a permit is issued, the owner/agent files a **certificate from an approved testing lab** showing the fabric meets **NFPA 701** (OFC 3104.2, Sherwood flyer). Confidence: H/M.
- **Action:** find the manufacturer's NFPA 701 / CPAI-84 certificate for your tent now. Keep a copy in the van, and look for the flame-resistance label sewn into the tent. Without it, a permit may be refused. (M)

**Typical permit conditions (Oregon district documents):**
- **20 ft from lot lines, buildings, other tents, parked vehicles and engines** (relevant to generators and vans). (M)
- No fireworks within 100 ft. (M)
- Anchoring and ballast to the manufacturer's spec. (M)
- No smoking. Fire extinguishers where required. Clear exits. (L)

**Lincoln County fire authorities:**
- **North Lincoln Fire & Rescue District #1** (Lincoln City area). Office of the Fire Marshal, 541-996-2233. Its tent document requires **15 days' advance notice** for tent permits. It also lists inflatables ("air supported domes, bounce houses") within its tent rules, so ask whether large balloon or air-supported structures are covered. (M/H)
- **Newport Fire Department, Central Oregon Coast Fire & Rescue (Waldport), Depoe Bay RFPD, Seal Rock RFPD, Yachats RFPD:** no tent pages found (gap). Assume a similar 2–3 week lead time and call.
- **Which authority applies.** The permit comes from whoever covers the *event address*, not from Poppies' home city.
- **Who pulls the permit.** Decide in each contract whether Poppies or the client/venue does, and who pays. Usually the tent owner/installer (Poppies) supplies the NFPA 701 certificate and site plan.

**Beaches and state parks (OPRD):**
- **The ocean shore is a state recreation area.** Commercial or "non-traditional" activity there requires an **OPRD special use permit** (OAR 736-021-0130, issued under OAR 736 division 16). (H)
- **Small weddings.** Per OPRD guidance (as reported in secondary sources), a beach wedding needs **no permit if under 50 guests and no structures**. **Tents, tables, chairs, arches, easels or banners trigger a permit.** **Every Poppies beach install (arches, the tent) is therefore a permit event.** (M)
- **Fees.** A **$100 non-refundable application fee** (OAR 736-016-0023) plus daily group-use fees. A Lincoln City resort quotes **$250** for a beach ceremony permit it arranges, which may include its markup. (H for the $100; L for the $250)
- **Lead time.** No official minimum found. One park guide says allow "at least a week". **Plan on 3–4+ weeks in peak season.** (L)
- **Contact:** OPRD Ocean Shores Permit Program, OceanShores.Permits@oprd.oregon.gov. (M)
- **No releases, no litter.** Balloon releases or debris on the beach would breach permit conditions and visitor-conduct rules (OAR 736-021-0100 bars altering property and structures). Build "everything leaves with us" into your process. (M)
- **City parks and city-owned beach accesses** may need a separate city park-use permit. Not researched (gap).

**Vacation rentals.** Separately check the rental's own rules. Many Lincoln County STR permits limit occupancy and events, and tents and helium may breach the host's rules. This is a contract point (§6), not a Poppies permit. (L)

## 6. Contracts

Every job should have a written agreement, even small ones. **Clauses common in balloon/event-decor contracts** (vendor templates; M for "common practice", not for enforceability):

- **Deposit / retainer and payment schedule.** E.g. 30–50% to book, balance due 7–14 days before the event, card or ACH. Name it a **"booking retainer that reserves the date and covers design time and materials ordered."**
- **Cancellation by client.** A tiered schedule: retainer kept; X% due if cancelled within 30 days; 100% within 7 days. Some vendors offer a credit toward a future event within 12 months instead of a refund.
- **Weather and outdoor installs.** Balloons are not guaranteed against heat, sun, wind, rain or cold. Add a coast-specific **wind-speed cut-off**: Poppies may decline or modify an outdoor install or tent above X mph. Require a client backup location. The client provides protection for outdoor pieces. Weather does not count as a client cancellation unless agreed.
- **Force majeure.** Covers illness, road closures (Hwy 101/18/20 slides) and venue closure. If you can't perform, refund minus costs or reschedule.
- **Damage.** The client is liable for damage to Poppies' rentals (frames, bases, tent). Poppies is liable only for damage it negligently causes during install. Decide whether to state that installs are freestanding and won't attach to walls without written permission.
- **Venue/host permission.** The client warrants the venue or rental allows the decor, tent, helium, stakes and access times, and that it will obtain or cover permits unless Poppies quotes for them.
- **Latex disclosure.** The client is told the balloons contain natural latex and is responsible for warning guests. Keep balloons away from young children (choking hazard). Offer foil/latex-free options.
- **No balloon release.** Poppies does not sell for release, the client agrees not to release balloons, and mylar near power lines is a hazard. Fits OPRD rules and the brand.
- **Removal / strike.** Specify the pick-up window, the fee for late access, and any items not returned.
- **Photo and marketing rights.** Poppies may photograph and post the installation. Opt-out for private clients. Don't post guests' faces without permission.
- **Limitation of liability.** Total liability capped at the contract price. No liability for consequential losses. **Don't exclude liability for your own negligence causing injury.** Courts may not enforce it, and it reads badly.
- **Change orders, substitutions** (colour or stock shortages), **governing law** (Oregon, Lincoln County courts), signatures.

**Oregon-specific points:**
- **Non-refundable deposits.** Oregon courts analyse "liquidated damages" with ORS 72.7180 as the starting point (*Illingworth v. Bushong*). A sum that is a reasonable pre-estimate of loss is enforceable. One that is **grossly disproportionate** to actual damages is an unenforceable penalty. *Zemp v. Rowland* held a non-refundable fee that is the *price of a right* (e.g. to reserve or exit) is not liquidated damages at all. (M: real case law, but real-estate and lease contexts)
- **Drafting implications.** Keep the retainer proportionate (it covers lost bookings, design time and materials ordered). Describe it as the price of reserving the date. Avoid letting yourself choose between keeping the deposit *or* suing for more. (L: inference for a lawyer to confirm)
- **Consumer protection (UTPA, ORS 646.608).** The statute lists ~79 unlawful practices. Consumers can sue for actual damages or **$200 statutory damages, whichever is greater**, and the AG and DAs can enforce. (H)
- **Avoiding UTPA trouble.** Advertise honestly ("eco-friendly", "biodegradable" claims are risky), state cancellation terms clearly *before* taking money, and refund promptly when the contract says so. I found nothing specifically on deposits under the UTPA (gap).
- **E-signatures.** Oregon's Uniform Electronic Transactions Act (ORS 84.019) says a signature or record can't be denied legal effect solely because it is electronic, and an e-signature satisfies a signature requirement, *where both parties agreed to transact electronically*. So an online contract signed through HoneyBook, Dubsado, Jotform, Square Contracts etc. is fine. Include a line saying the parties agree to sign electronically. (H)

## Questions for a lawyer / accountant / insurer

**Lawyer (Oregon business / contracts):**
1. Given the POPPIE USPTO registration for balloon design services, should we register any name with SOS before resolving naming? Is an ABN filing any defence at all?
2. LLC vs sole prop for a 1–2 person installer that puts up an 800 sq ft tent on beaches and at rentals. Does an LLC materially help, given we'll carry GL?
3. Is our tiered retainer and cancellation schedule enforceable under *Illingworth* / *Zemp*? What wording frames it as the price of reserving the date? Any UTPA disclosure requirements before taking a deposit?
4. Can we enforceably require the client to warrant venue/STR permission and to indemnify us if the host objects to the tent or helium?
5. Wording for a limitation-of-liability clause and weather/wind cut-off clause that Oregon courts will respect for consumer clients.
6. If a friend helps on install days, are they an employee? What triggers workers' comp?

**Accountant (CPA/EA):**
1. Is buying balloons for installations "resale" or "supplies" for the purposes of out-of-state supplier exemptions and the Oregon resale certificate?
2. First-year estimated tax plan: safe-harbour approach given no prior-year self-employment income?
3. Vehicle: standard mileage vs actual expense. How do we document trips between coast cities?
