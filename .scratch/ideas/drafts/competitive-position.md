# Competitive position, on one page

*A synthesis of `../research/08-competitor-teardown.md`, which has the full
profiles and the comparison matrix. Search snippets only; **phone-check
everything in the last section before relying on it.***

## The position in one line

> **The only balloon installation studio actually on the central coast:
> coast-proofed, quick to book, priced in the open, and gallery-quiet in
> style.**

Every installation rival lives 1.5–2.5 hours away. The one local balloon shop,
Float Balloon Bar LC in Lincoln City, sells gift bouquets and garlands, and
its status is unclear.

## The five moves that matter most

1. **Publish starting prices.** The valley anchors are already visible ($180+
   from Balloon Theories; a $250 minimum and about $25/ft from Pop & Go), and
   no coast vendor publishes install prices. (Ideas #21, #72.)
2. **Say the geography out loud:** "Free installation from Lincoln City to
   Yachats. Booked in a week, not two." (Ideas #26, #140.)
3. **Win local search early**, before the lead-gen pages settle in: the
   Google profile, plus one page per town. (Ideas #9, #36.)
4. **Out-review everyone in year one.** Most rivals have 0–5 reviews; 30
   steady Google reviews would lead the region. (Ideas #60–#63.)
5. **Turn the locals into partners**: florists, rental firms, the gift bar,
   venues and property managers. (Ideas #131, #135, #136.)

## Where Poppies will lose, and should let it go

- **Budget clients.** $35–50 florist bouquets and $75 garlands from Portland
  part-timers. Don't chase them.
- **Very large builds** that need a team on several weekends at once, until
  she has help.
- **Walk-in impulse buys.** There's no storefront, unless the pop-up idea
  (#92) happens.
