# Review kit: asking for reviews, the right way

*Ready-to-use messages for the moment after every install. Every one is written
to stay inside Google's review policy and the FTC's 2024 Consumer Review Rule.
The rules are summarised at the bottom; the full sourcing is in
`../research/04-reviews-and-local-marketing.md` §2 and "Rules not to break".*

**The pattern, for every client, every time.** Asking everyone the same way is
the rule. Picking only happy clients ("gating") is banned.

1. **At the reveal or teardown:** a soft verbal ask, and leave the card.
2. **Within 24–48 hours:** the "your photos" message, with the review link
   underneath.
3. **Days 7–10:** one reminder. Then stop.
4. **Weddings only:** one more gentle note once the couple has their
   photographer's gallery.

Replace `[REVIEW LINK]` with the official link from the Google Business
Profile (Read reviews → Get more reviews → Share). The QR code version is
available there on desktop.

---

## 1. The card left at the install

*Printed, about A6, on the brand's warm off-white. One side only.*

> **Thank you for having us.**
>
> If you have a moment after the celebration, a few words about your
> experience help other families on the coast find us.
>
> [QR CODE]  `[REVIEW LINK]`
>
> *Poppies — Events that pop.*

The verbal ask that goes with it is one sentence, said once, to everyone:

> "If you're happy to, there's a card there with a link for a review. No
> pressure at all."

## 2. The "your photos" message (24–48 hours after)

*Send the photos no matter what. They must never depend on a review, because
that would count as an incentive.*

**Subject:** Your photos from [event, e.g. "Maya's first birthday"]

> Hi [Name],
>
> Here are the photos of your installation: [GALLERY LINK]. They're yours to
> keep and share. If you post any, we'd love to be tagged @[handle], but
> only if you'd like to.
>
> Thank you again for having us. It was a lovely one to build.
>
> If you have a minute, a short review helps other people on the coast find
> us: [REVIEW LINK]
>
> [Her name]
> Poppies

## 3. One reminder (days 7–10)

> Hi [Name], a quick note in case it got buried: if you'd like to leave a few
> words about your installation, here's the link: [REVIEW LINK]. Either way,
> thank you, and I hope the celebration was everything you wanted.
>
> [Her name]

Then stop. Don't send a third ask.

## 4. Weddings: after the photographer's gallery arrives

> Hi [Name] and [Name], I hope married life is treating you well. When your
> photographer's gallery comes through, I'd love to see how the arch looked
> on the day. If you feel like writing a few words about working with us,
> here's the link: [REVIEW LINK]

## 5. Replying to reviews

Reply to **every** review, briefly, in the brand's voice. Never argue, never
threaten, never ask anyone to change or remove a review.

**A warm review**

> Thank you, [first name]. It was a joy to build [what it was] for [the
> occasion], and the [detail they mentioned] was our favourite part too.

**A critical review**

> Thank you for telling us, [first name]. I'm sorry [the specific thing]
> fell short. I'd like to understand what happened; please reach me at
> [email]. [One factual, non-defensive sentence about what's changed, if
> anything has.]

Then contact them privately. Don't offer anything in exchange for changing the
review.

## 6. Measuring it

- Add a required **"How did you hear about us?"** dropdown to the enquiry form:
  Google, Instagram, Facebook, Nextdoor, a venue, a planner or photographer,
  a friend, saw an installation, other. (That's a small code change to the
  form field table.)
- Tag the QR card link: `?utm_source=qr&utm_campaign=install-card`.
- Track reviews per month on each platform. **The goal is 1–2 new Google
  reviews a month, steadily.** A launch-week burst can get reviews filtered or
  paused.

## The rules these messages follow

- **No incentives of any kind** for a review: no discount, freebie, raffle or
  "free refresh". Google bans all incentives; the FTC bans those tied to
  sentiment. Follow the stricter rule, Google's.
- **No gating.** Ask every client, the same way.
- **No scripting.** Don't suggest a star rating or wording. "A few words about
  your experience" is fine.
- **No insider reviews.** Her family, friends doing her a favour, or anyone
  who works with her must not post as customers.
- **No suppression.** Nothing in contracts that discourages reviews.
- **Don't ask for Yelp reviews** at all; Yelp forbids it.
- **No bursts.** A steady trickle beats a launch-week blitz.
