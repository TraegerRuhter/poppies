# The first 90 days

From 11 November 2026 to Valentine's weekend. The aim: **legal, photographed,
findable, and five paid jobs with five reviews.** Each week has one thing
that matters most, marked ★. "Dev" means the developer or Claude can do it
without her.

The order is deliberate. Nothing is bought under the name until the trademark
is checked. Nothing is sold until she's insured. Nothing is pitched until
there are photos.

## Weeks 1–2 (11–24 Nov): unblock

- ★ **Check the name.** Look up USPTO serial 98162236 (POPPIE) at
  tsdr.uspto.gov, and search the Oregon Secretary of State business registry
  for "Poppies". If POPPIE is live, book one hour with a trademark lawyer
  before anything else.
- **Answer the twelve calls** in `decisions-for-her.md`. Twenty minutes, on
  the plan page.
- **Book the free session** at the Oregon Coast Community College Small
  Business Development Center. Bring `the-plan.md` and the startup checklist.
- **Phone-check three facts:** is Float Balloon Bar in Lincoln City open and
  would it partner; what Oregon Coast Event Rental charges for a 20×40 tent;
  what Salem Balloons and Flowers charges to come to the coast.
- **Find the tent's flame-resistance certificate** (NFPA 701), or ask the
  maker for a copy.
- Dev: set the enquiry form's email settings in Vercel once she has an
  address to receive them.

## Weeks 3–4 (25 Nov–8 Dec): make it official

- ★ **Register.** Sole proprietor or LLC (the SBDC will help choose), the
  assumed business name, an EIN, a business bank account. About $50–150.
- **Insurance.** $1M/$2M general liability as a decor installer, with the
  tent disclosed. Ask about same-day certificates naming a venue.
- **Home-city business licence.**
- **Buy the starter kit** and open a wholesale account: inflator, hand
  pumps, sizer, frames, weights, and opening stock in **one palette** to
  start. $750 lean.
- **Time one build.** Make a 10 foot garland at home with a stopwatch. That
  number replaces the model's guess of 16.5 minutes a foot.

## Weeks 5–7 (9–29 Dec): build the proof

The holidays are the best free backdrop of the year. Use them for photos, not
for selling.

- ★ **Three portfolio pieces, photographed in daylight before anyone
  arrives:**
  1. A holiday window for a shop she likes, as a gift. Ask only for a credit
     card in the window and permission to photograph.
  2. An Arrival in a real rental house, arranged with one property manager.
     This doubles as the first pitch.
  3. A sculpture: one ocean creature, shot against a clean wall.
- **Work through `/shot-list`** on the site with those three builds. Aim for
  30 usable photos.
- **Draft the contract** from `../ideas/research/10`, and book one hour with
  an Oregon lawyer to review it.
- **Set up payments:** Square or Stripe, for deposits.
- Dev: put the photos in the site. Publish the menu and "from" prices once
  she's agreed them. Remove the homepage sketches.

## Weeks 8–10 (30 Dec–19 Jan): get found

- ★ **Google Business Profile live** (checklist in
  `../ideas/drafts/google-business-profile-setup.md`), with all 30 photos.
- **Buy the domain** in her name. Dev: connect it, verify email sending, set
  the public address.
- **Claim the free listings:** The Knot, WeddingWire, Zola, Nextdoor, Apple,
  Bing.
- **Tell everyone she knows.** A short personal note with the site link and
  one photo. The first five jobs usually come from people who already like
  you.
- **Pitch three property managers** before spring-break bookings start. Use
  the Arrival photos and the one-page offer in
  `../ideas/drafts/partner-outreach.md`.
- Dev: publish the four explainers as guides, the "how it works" page and
  the FAQ.

## Weeks 11–13 (20 Jan–14 Feb): first paid work

- ★ **Sell Valentine's.** The Arrival and The Question are made for it.
  One post, one email to the property managers, one note to coast
  photographers who shoot proposals.
- **Run the review kit on every job:** the card at the install, the photos
  message within two days, one reminder.
- **Plan the March styled shoot:** one photographer, one florist, one venue,
  one planner. Everyone gets the images.
- **Pitch one public piece** for the June kite festival to Explore Lincoln
  City. March is when they plan.
- **Walk into ten shops** with a mocked-up photo of each one's window, and
  offer The Standing Order from March.

## Day 90: the scoreboard

| Target | Why it matters |

| Name cleared, registered, insured | Venues and property managers will ask |
| 30 photographs on the site | Photos are the binding constraint on everything |
| Prices published | No coast rival has done it |

If the name check goes badly, everything after week 2 still holds. Only the
name on the paperwork changes.
