## What the year earns

From `numbers.py`. Menu prices, $4,000 of yearly fixed costs, 27.5% of profit
set aside for tax, and six unpaid hours a week for quoting, messages and
outreach.

| Headland arches | 6 | 8 | 10 |
| Arrivals | 12 | 30 | 60 |

| Shop visits (Standing Order months) | 12 | 36 | 72 |
| **Revenue** | **$32,400** | **$67,900** | **$118,100** |
| Profit before tax | $20,900 | $48,100 | $86,400 |
| **Take-home after tax set-aside** | **$15,100** | **$34,900** | **$62,600** |
| Hours a week on jobs | 8 | 17 | 29 |
| Hours a week in all | 14 | 23 | 35 |
| Take-home per hour, all hours counted | $21 | $31 | $36 |

**What to read from it:**

- The careful year is a paid apprenticeship in her own business: one job a
  week, about $15,000 kept, and every estimate in the model replaced with a
  real number.
- The gap between careful and expected is mostly **The Arrival**. Eighteen
  more of them is the property-manager channel working.
- The gap between expected and strong is **The Pavilion and the shops**.
  Three more tent weekends add about $8,500 of contribution for 75 hours.
- Take-home per hour rises with volume because the unpaid hours stay about
  the same.

| The Headland | $550 | $398 | 7¾ | **$51** |
| Collected | $136 | $114 | 2½ | **$47** |

"She keeps" is the price less materials, mileage at 76¢ a mile and 3% card
fees. Hours include a 25 mile drive each way and the return trip for
teardown.

**Three ways to earn more without working more,** in order of effect:

1. **Batch the drive.** Two jobs in one town on one day removes an hour of
   driving and $38 of mileage from each.
2. **Sell the teardown or skip it.** Collected has no drive at all.
3. **Say yes to The Pavilion.** One tent weekend earns what five garlands do.
