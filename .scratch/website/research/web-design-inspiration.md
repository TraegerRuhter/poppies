Date: 2026-11-07

### Synthesis: what makes these memorable (and still useful)

1. **One strong idea carried consistently**, not many effects. Flower Dose organises by occasion. Blossomtype makes the material the type. East Fork uses voice. (Medium, editorial judgement)
2. **Photography first, chrome quiet.** This repeats across florist roundups: "clean, minimalist look with strong photography" ([Sitebuilder Report](https://www.sitebuilderreport.com/inspiration/florist-and-flower-shop-websites), [Zarla](https://www.zarla.com/inspiration/florist), [HubSpot](https://blog.hubspot.com/website/florist-websites)). Poppies' §8 already commits to this. (Medium)
3. **The maker is visible.** Solo-studio sites that win put the person on the page (CaiYawen's about page). (Medium)
4. **Awards reward usability too.** The hotel and bakery recognitions above cite booking flow and usability, not just spectacle. (Medium)

## 2. Playful-but-useful interaction patterns

| Pattern | Evidence of usefulness / conversion | Pitfalls | Confidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Mad-Libs / sentence form** ("I'm planning a ___ for ___ people in ___") | LukeW reported that Vast.com's A/B test of narrative forms "increased conversion across the board by 25–40%" ([Konigi summary](https://konigi.com/blog/mad-libs-style-form-increases-conversion-25-40), [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/using-natural-language-to-improve-conversion-rates-41632)). Konigi notes that many variables changed at once. Patrick McKenzie's test went the *other* way, though his form was harder to parse (same source). | Screen readers. Adrian Roselli: sentence forms "have a screen reader interpretation problem". Each blank needs a real programmatic label, and the sentence needs a group label ([Roselli, "Sentence Forms (not Mad Libs)"](https://adrianroselli.com/2021/08/sentence-forms-not-mad-libs.html)). Wrapping breaks badly on phones. Placeholders are not labels ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/mobile-forms-avoid-inline-labels)). | Medium (the 25–40% figure is 2010-era and secondhand) |
| **Conversational / one-question-at-a-time forms** (Typeform style) | Typeform *claims* higher completion. A controlled academic study (N=100 per arm) found the conversational form rated **more interesting** but did not measure conversion ([arXiv 2003.02537](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.02537)). Vendor numbers conflict: one claims a 4x lift, while another single-company test found a form converting 36% vs 13% for a chatbot ([Perspective](https://getperspective.ai/blog/the-conversion-gap-between-forms-and-conversations-hit-4x-in-2026), [Orbit Forms](https://orbitforms.ai/blog/conversational-forms-vs-traditional-forms)). | Needs JS. Hides the total length. Hard to review answers. Third-party embeds are heavy. | Low |
| **Estimators / configurators / price calculators** | Vendor case studies claim large lifts: VenturePact +28% conversion, Sisu +200% leads ([Outgrow](https://outgrow.co/blog/interactive-calculators-lead-generation-conversion), [casestudies.com](https://www.casestudies.com/company/outgrow/case-study/sisu-generated-200-more-leads-with-the-help-of-outgrows-interactive-content)). No controls or sample sizes are given. In the balloon trade specifically, **[Fanfaire](https://www.fanfaire.io/design-studio)** sells a "design studio" with real balloon photos and a pricing engine that clients use to "tweak colors, styles, and add-ons in real time" ([Fanfaire storefront](https://www.fanfaire.io/digital-storefront)). That is evidence the category is moving toward self-serve visual quoting. | It implies fixed prices. decisions.md §3 rejects a booking calendar for the same reason. A calculator that shows one number becomes a promise. Gating the result behind contact details (the vendor advice) feels manipulative on a craft site. | Low (conversion), Medium (Fanfaire exists) |
| **Published "from" pricing** (static) | Not an interaction, but it does the estimator's job with no JS. Balloon studios publish per-foot garland prices of roughly **$18–$45/ft** with minimums and separate install fees ([inflatelouisville](https://inflatelouisville.squarespace.com/decor), [funandconfetti](https://funandconfetti.squarespace.com/balloo-services), [Bonjour Fête help](https://bonjour-fete-lzqcbxzgfja.gorgias.help/en-US/how-much-do-balloon-garlands-cost-1324471)). | It can anchor too low if the offering model has not settled (it is still open in decisions.md). | Medium |
| **Colour / palette pickers** | In this category the palette *is* the brief. Papier's live preview of personalised stationery is the nearest proven precedent for "see your choices before you order" ([startup-seeker](https://startup-seeker.com/company/papier~com)). I found no official interactive Sempertex/Tuftex chart. The results were spam pages. | Colour alone fails users with colour-vision deficiency, so every chip needs a visible name. Screen colour is not latex colour, so it needs a "colours confirmed in person/sample" note. | Low–Medium |
| **Before/after sliders** (empty venue, then installed) | Proves transformation. Strongest when the venue is a real local one. | Use a native `<input type="range">` driving a `clip-path`, which gives keyboard and ARIA behaviour almost for free ([freefrontend](https://freefrontend.com/css-image-comparison-sliders/), [svelte-before-after](https://sveltethemes.dev/Jaydeep189/svelte-before-after)). It needs matched photo pairs, which is a shot-list item. | Medium |
| **Scroll-driven storytelling** | NN/g: scrolljacking "threatens user control, discoverability, attention, efficiency, and task success". Every participant on a scrolljacked Crypto.com page was disoriented. It was tolerated best when it "progressively disclosed relevant information" ([NN/g Scrolljacking 101](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolljacking-101/)). Parallax: users rated it more fun but found no usability gain, and some got motion sickness ([NN/g parallax](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/parallax-usability/), [UXPin summary of JUS study](https://www.uxpin.com/studio/blog/3-crucial-ux-considerations-going-parallax/)). | Never take over scroll speed. Scroll-*linked* fades (CSS) are fine. Scroll-*jacking* is not. | High (NN/g) |
| **"Museum label" / catalogue captions** | No conversion study found. Its value is that it answers buyer questions (size, venue, colours, scale) inside the gallery. It is a natural fit with the "gallery-wall" aesthetic. | Labels must be real text, not baked into images. Too much metadata turns the gallery into a spreadsheet. | Low (no direct evidence) |
| **Tactile / physics effects** (draggable objects, cursor followers) | Delight, plus portfolio fame for some [memory: Bruno Simon's drivable portfolio]. No evidence they help a service business convert. | NN/g: moving elements pull attention from the primary task and can be mistaken for ads ([NN/g UX animations](https://nngroup.com/videos/ux-animations), secondary summaries). JS weight. Vestibular triggers. **Floating balloons directly breaks decisions.md §8.** | Medium |
| **Custom cursors** | Nothing credible found either way. | On touch devices, which most of this audience uses, they do not exist. They often hide the system cursor. "Mystery meat" hover-only reveals hurt discoverability ([Wikipedia: mystery meat navigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mystery_meat_navigation)). | Low |
| **Easter eggs** | Free to add if static (a 404 page, a footer line). No conversion effect expected. | They must never sit in the enquiry path. | Low |
| **Availability displays** | A static "Now booking…" line gives urgency without a calendar. | It goes stale, which is the same failure decisions.md §3 found with calendars. Needs an "updated" date and a single data-file source. | Low (judgement) |
| **Carousels** | NN/g: carousel content is often ignored. Baymard: homepage carousels work only if 10 requirements are met ([NN/g video](https://www.nngroup.com/videos/carousels-websites-mobile-apps/), [Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/external-article-homepage-carousels)). | Avoid autoplay. | Medium |

### Underlying research on delight vs conversion

- **Aesthetic-usability effect (NN/g):** attractive designs are *perceived* as more usable and buy tolerance for minor problems, but not for serious ones ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/aesthetic-usability-effect/)). Delight is a multiplier on a working form, not a substitute for one. (Medium: snippet only)
- **Animation (NN/g):** consider "its goal, its frequency of occurrence, and its mechanics". Motion is "a powerful tool to attract users' attention" and that is exactly why gratuitous motion distracts ([NN/g UX animations](https://nngroup.com/videos/ux-animations), via secondary summaries). Durations commonly attributed to NN/g are about 100–500 ms, but I could not confirm the primary article ([NN/g animation duration](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/animation-duration/), unverified). (Medium)
- **Forms (Baymard):** field count matters more than step count. The average checkout has 11.3 fields when about 8 are needed ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/checkout-flow-average-form-fields), [Baymard checkout guide](https://baymard.com/blog/checkout-flow-ux-optimization)). Keep labels visible. Placeholders are for examples only ([Baymard mobile labels](https://baymard.com/blog/mobile-forms-avoid-inline-labels)). Inline validation helps but does not replace error handling on submit ([Baymard inline validation](https://baymard.com/blog/inline-form-validation)). These are e-commerce studies, but they transfer to enquiry forms. (High/Medium)

## 4. Constraints

### Accessibility (WCAG 2.2)
- WCAG 2.2 became a W3C Recommendation on **5 Oct 2023** and adds nine criteria ([W3C What's new](https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/new-in-22/)). Relevant here (High):
  - **2.4.11 Focus Not Obscured (Minimum), AA.** Sticky headers and sticky "Enquire" bars must not cover the focused element ([WebAIM](https://webaim.org/standards/wcag/wcag22)).
  - **2.5.8 Target Size (Minimum), AA.** 24×24 CSS px, or enough spacing around the target. Colour chips and gallery dots are the risk ([WebAIM](https://webaim.org/standards/wcag/wcag22), [Deque](https://dequeuniversity.com/rules/axe/4.8/target-size)).
  - **2.4.7 Focus Visible** (existing) and the **2.4.13 Focus Appearance** (AAA) direction. A designed focus ring in the accent colour is a chance for brand delight.
- **2.3.3 Animation from Interactions (AAA).** Interaction-triggered motion must be disable-able. `prefers-reduced-motion` is W3C's sufficient technique **C39** ([W3C C39](https://w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/css/C39)). JS-driven motion needs its own `matchMedia` check, because CSS alone does not cover it ([JM Family DS](https://webgency.github.io/design-system/foundations/anti-patterns/animation-without-reduced-motion)). (High)
- Vestibular disorders: scroll-linked parallax and large-scale motion are the known triggers ([U-Michigan](https://teamdynamix.umich.edu/TDClient/30/Portal/KB/Article/14019/Accommodating-Vertigo-and-Vestibular-Disorders), NN/g parallax above). Poppies' "quiet fades and rises" rule already sits in the safe zone. Under reduced motion, make even those instant. (High)
- Forms: every field needs a programmatic label, and sentence forms need a group label (Roselli, above). (High)

### Mobile performance
- **Core Web Vitals "good" thresholds, at the 75th percentile:** LCP ≤ 2.5 s, INP ≤ 200 ms, CLS ≤ 0.1 ([web.dev thresholds](https://web.dev/articles/defining-core-web-vitals-thresholds)). The same thresholds apply on mobile and desktop. (High)
- **Context:** the median mobile page is about 2.3–2.6 MB, with JS about 630–700 KB and images about 0.9 MB (HTTP Archive 2025 via secondary sources: [CSS-Tricks repost](https://frontend-feeds.netlify.app/css-tricks/http-archive-2025-web-almanac), [Unlighthouse](https://unlighthouse.dev/tools/page-size); the primary is [Web Almanac 2025 page weight](https://almanac.httparchive.org/en/2025/page-weight)). A brochure site with zero JS and AVIF/WebP images can beat the median several times over, which matters on coast cell service. (Medium: figures conflict)
- **Astro islands:** zero client JS by default. `client:visible` hydrates only when the component scrolls into view (IntersectionObserver). `client:idle` is for non-critical UI ([Astro islands docs mirror](https://v4.docs.astro.build/ar/concepts/islands/)). Any playful widget (estimator, slider) should be an island hydrated with `client:visible`, or better still, written in plain CSS/HTML with no hydration at all. (Medium: mirror, not canonical)
- **Budget suggestion (judgement):** gallery hero LCP image under about 150 KB AVIF at phone width. Total JS on any page under about 20 KB, and 0 KB on the homepage. One variable font file plus at most one more.

## 5. Trends 2025–2026: durable vs fad

| Trend | Status | Verdict for Poppies | Confidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **CSS scroll-driven animations** (`animation-timeline: view()/scroll()`) | Chrome/Edge 115+, **Safari 26+**, Firefox behind a flag or in Technology Preview per Chrome's docs ([Chrome dev docs](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/css-ui/scroll-driven-animations)). Same-document VT is an Interop 2026 area. Firefox users just get a normal navigation. Ideal for the doorway → path handoff. | High |
| **Variable fonts** | Mature | **Durable.** One file, optical sizes. A soft, round-shouldered grotesk can say "balloon" without script lettering. | Medium |
| **Kinetic typography** | Popular in 2026 trend pieces. The best versions "play once…then settle" ([Envato](https://elements.envato.com/learn/web-design-trends), [Line25](https://line25.com/articles/web-design-trends-2026/)) | Mostly a **fad** at full strength. A one-time settle on the doorway question is acceptable *if* it is a fade/rise. | Low |
| **Tactile brutalism / anti-design / neubrutalism** | Widely touted for 2026. Even its boosters say it suits "portfolios, studios, brands selling personality" and that "anti-design that reads as careless is just a broken website" ([Fireart](https://fireart.studio/blog/the-best-web-design-trends/), [Plaxonic](https://plaxonic.com/insights/blog/2026-web-design-trends)) | **Fad for this brand.** It fights the gallery-wall decision and reads as careless to wedding clients. Borrow only the *tactile* part: paper-like grain and honest material photos. | Medium |
| **Bento grids** | Widest adoption, lowest barrier ([Plaxonic](https://plaxonic.com/insights/blog/2026-web-design-trends)) | **Durable, but generic.** Useful for a services overview. Risky for the gallery, because a bento needs enough images to fill it, which conflicts with decisions.md §2. | Medium |
| **AI personalisation, dark mode** | Trend lists | Not relevant. Dark mode works against warm-neutral photography. | Low |

## Clichés to avoid (balloon and event sites)

Mostly editorial judgement from category scans ([onemillionbubbles roundup](https://onemillionbubbles.org/my-post), local directories, Squarespace/Jotform pages found above), reinforced by decisions.md §8. Confidence: Medium/Low.

1. **Rainbow or primary-colour chrome**, and poppy red or hot pink as a field colour. It competes with the photos and reads as budget party-supply (decisions.md §8).
2. **Script or handwriting logos and headings**, "balloon-letter" bubble fonts, and Comic-adjacent rounded fonts.
3. **Floating, bouncing or drifting balloon animations, and confetti bursts on submit.** The single most common "fun" move, and the one decisions.md forbids.
4. **Clip-art or vector balloon icons** in place of real photographs.
5. **Mid-party photos with clutter**: party-store backdrops, mixed lighting, watermarks across the work.
6. **"DM for pricing" / Instagram-only galleries / embedded IG feeds.** Slow, privacy-heavy, and they leave Google with nothing to index.
7. **Autoplay hero carousels / slideshows** (NN/g and Baymard, above).
8. **Jotform or Google Forms iframes** with 20 fields and no sense of the brand.
9. **Pun overload.** One good line ("Events that pop") beats a pun in every heading.
10. **Rose-gold "luxe" everything.** The default upmarket cue in the trade, now generic.
11. **Kids'-party clip-art register and licensed characters** in heroes (decisions.md §2).
12. **Pop-up modals and chat widgets** on arrival.
13. **Templated Canva/Squarespace "balloon business" layouts.** There are Etsy and Gumroad templates for exactly this ([Etsy](https://www.etsy.com/market/balloon_portfolio), [Gumroad](https://balloonbizmentor.gumroad.com/l/websitetemplate)), and the sameness shows.

## Patterns that fit Poppies

Each pattern has a one-line idea, why it helps, and the constraint it must respect. **⚠ BENDS RULE** marks anything that strains the committed rules (no bouncing or floating, quiet fades and rises only, no script fonts, no calendar).

1. **Sentence-form enquiry.** "I'm planning a [celebration ▾] for about [40] people in [Manzanita] on [date]." *Why:* the fun lives in the copy and adds no JS. The 25–40% Mad-Libs lift is a weak but positive signal. *Constraint:* real `<label>`s (they can be visually integrated) plus a `<fieldset>/<legend>`. Collapse to stacked fields below about 400 px. Follow Roselli's guidance.
2. **Doorway handoff with native cross-document View Transitions.** The chosen Celebrations/Weddings photo tile becomes the path page's hero. *Why:* continuity makes the branch feel like walking through a door, and it is pure CSS. *Constraint:* a crossfade or rise only, no zoom swoops. Turn it off under `prefers-reduced-motion`. Firefox gets a normal load.
3. **Scroll-linked "quiet rise" for gallery photos** using `animation-timeline: view()`: 8–16 px translate plus opacity. *Why:* the gallery feels alive with zero JS. *Constraint:* content is visible by default (progressive enhancement), the effect is fully disabled under reduced motion, and scroll speed is never changed (NN/g scrolljacking).
4. **Museum-label captions.** Under each photo, in small caps: *Garland, 24 ft · Cannon Beach backyard · 6 colours · installed in 3 h*. *Why:* it answers scale, place and price-shaping questions inside the proof, and suits the gallery-wall look. *Constraint:* real text in content collections. Keep to 1–2 lines.
5. **Palette chips from her actual stock**, as named swatches (e.g. "Sand", "Blush", "Sea glass"). Up to 4 can be ticked, and they prefill the enquiry's colours field. *Why:* the palette *is* the brief, and it is the most delightful useful thing a balloon site can offer. *Constraint:* `<input type=checkbox>` with visible names (no colour-only meaning), targets of 24 px or more (2.5.8), and a "colours confirmed on a physical sample" note. CSS-only (`:has()`), or a tiny island.
6. **Carry choices into the form through the URL**, with plain links like `/enquire?path=weddings&colours=sand,sea-glass`. *Why:* enquiries arrive pre-qualified (decisions.md §7) with no state library. *Constraint:* a few lines of progressive-enhancement JS. The form must still work blank.
7. **Scale chooser as photo radio cards**: "A touch" / "A statement" / "The whole room", each with a real photo and a "from $" band. *Why:* it replaces the "rough scale" text field with something visual and anchors budget softly (§3). *Constraint:* native radios. Needs at least one photo per tier, so it goes on the shot list.
8. **Static "from" pricing plus a typical range** ("Most celebrations land between $X and $Y"). *Why:* it does 80% of an estimator's job with 0 KB of JS and prevents tyre-kickers. *Constraint:* blocked on the offering model. No single-number promises.
9. **Optional estimator island (later).** A garland-length slider gives a price *range* plus install fee. *Why:* the category is moving toward self-serve quoting (Fanfaire). *Constraint:* `client:visible`, under 10 KB. Always a range with a "your quote confirms" note. **⚠ Bends §3's spirit** (it implies standard pricing). Revisit only if a standard product exists.
10. **Before/after slider** for empty venue vs installed, at real coast venues. *Why:* proves transformation and builds local SEO relevance. *Constraint:* native `<input type=range>` plus `clip-path`, a visible focus ring, and a label. Only where matched shots exist.
11. **Honest availability line** in the header or form intro: "Now booking spring & summer 2027 · updated 1 Nov". *Why:* urgency and reassurance without a calendar. *Constraint:* one data-file value with a visible "updated" date. Remove it rather than let it go stale. **⚠ Edges toward §3.** Keep it vague (seasons, not dates).
12. **Service-area map as a quiet SVG coastline** with named towns and travel-fee bands. *Why:* heads off quotes from three hours inland (§7) and helps local SEO. *Constraint:* static inline SVG under 10 KB, and the same list as text for screen readers and search.
13. **Tent-scale diagram.** A simple line drawing of the 20×40 tent with seated/standing capacities and a person for scale. *Why:* answers the first tent question before it is asked. *Constraint:* static SVG with alt text, in the accent colour only.
14. **"How it works" four-step strip:** Enquire → Palette & sketch → Install day → We collect. *Why:* remote bookers from Portland need to know she handles it end to end. *Constraint:* plain HTML. No step animation beyond a fade.
15. **FAQ as `<details>/<summary>`** (Do you deliver to ___? Indoor vs outdoor? How long do they last? Helium?). *Why:* useful, keyboard-accessible, zero JS. *Constraint:* real headings for SEO.
16. **Poppy-red focus ring and form-success state as the brand's signature moments.** *Why:* the accent "only reads as vivid if surrounded by restraint" (§8), and using it for focus makes accessibility the place it shows. *Constraint:* ≥3:1 contrast against the warm ground, never obscured by sticky UI (2.4.11).
17. **Sticky bottom "Enquire" bar on path pages (mobile only)**, appearing after the first gallery screen. *Why:* phone-first conversion. *Constraint:* must not cover focused fields (2.4.11), targets ≥44 px, hidden on the form page.
18. **Copy voice as the delight** (East Fork lesson). First person, warm, specific ("I'll come by the morning of and be gone before the guests arrive"). Success message: "Sent. I'll reply within a day." *Constraint:* one pun per page, at most.
19. **Balloon-photo letterforms for one display word** (Blossomtype idea): the "pop" in the tagline set as cut-out real balloon photography, once, on the homepage. *Why:* memorable and on-brand, and literally the work. *Constraint:* an SVG/AVIF image with proper alt text, not a font, so the no-script rule is unaffected.
20. **Soft-shouldered variable sans as the type voice** (rounded-terminal grotesk at large display sizes, neutral text face). *Why:* hints at inflation and roundness without cutesy script. *Constraint:* one variable file, `font-display: swap`, subset. Rounded faces can tip into kidsy, so test on the wedding path.
21. **Path temperature shift through CSS custom properties.** Celebrations warmer, Weddings cooler and coastal (§8), switched by one `data-path` attribute. *Why:* the visitor feels the doorway choice. *Constraint:* both themes must pass contrast. No palette swap, just temperature.
22. **Tasteful 404:** "This page popped." with one still photo of a single deflated balloon and links back to both paths. *Why:* a harmless easter egg. *Constraint:* static, no animation.
23. **Matte paper grain on the ground colour** (an inline SVG noise layer at 2–4% opacity). *Why:* a "tactile" 2026 cue that warms the gallery wall. *Constraint:* under 2 KB, `background` only, and it must not reduce text contrast.
24. **"Delivered, not shipped" proof row**: three testimonials, each paired with the photo of that event and its town. *Why:* trust for remote bookers. *Constraint:* real, attributed quotes only.
25. **Draggable or floating balloon toy** (e.g. on the 404 or as a cursor follower). **⚠ BENDS RULE: rejected.** Floating or bouncing breaks §8, adds JS and vestibular risk, and does nothing on touch. Listed only so it is consciously ruled out.
